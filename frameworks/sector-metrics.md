# Sector Metrics and Valuation Method Selection

## 1. Classify the economic model

Identify who pays, what is sold, how revenue repeats, what capital is required and who bears financing or underwriting risk. Classify by actual operations, not the stock-market label. Separate materially different segments; a retailer with a lending subsidiary needs both operating and credit analysis.

Select a small set covering growth, earning quality, capital efficiency, survival and valuation. Compare consistent definitions across several years and comparable peers. Record currency, accounting differences, lease treatment and cycle position. Treat missing disclosure as a limitation, not zero risk.

## 2. Choose the appropriate earnings and value basis

- **Operating businesses:** distinguish operating profit from cash available after tax, asset replacement and working-capital investment. Use normalized earnings or cash flow, not a peak or an unexplained adjusted figure.
- **Banks and insurers:** examine common-equity earning power and capital available for distribution after regulatory needs. Use equity cash-flow, dividend or excess-return approaches when appropriate; do not apply ordinary enterprise debt subtraction to deposits and policyholder funding.
- **Asset-backed businesses:** value assets net of associated claims and required spending. Reconcile income-based and asset-based estimates; do not add both as separate assets.
- **Loss-making or rapidly changing businesses:** use unit economics, liquidity runway and explicit profitability assumptions. Revenue multiples are comparison tools, not evidence of intrinsic value.

Match numerator and denominator: equity value with earnings/cash attributable to equity; enterprise value with operating earnings/cash before financing. A multiple must use the same accounting basis as its peers. Do not sum accounting profits as distributable cash without reconciling reinvestment and financing needs.

## 3. Use the industry guide

Select the relevant row or rows. Prioritize the economic drivers and traps; do not calculate every ratio merely because it is available.

| Business model | Growth and quality measures | Valuation measures | Important checks / misleading shortcuts |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Industrials and business services | Organic sales split into price/volume; margins; return on invested capital; backlog conversion; cash conversion | Normalized operating earnings and cash flow; enterprise value / operating profit | Customer concentration, contract losses, working capital and replacement investment. Backlog is not assured revenue; acquired growth is not organic growth. |
| Consumer brands and staples | Volume versus pricing; repeat demand; market share; gross profit and capital returns | Normalized equity earnings, cash yield, operating-profit multiples | Brand spending, distribution dependence, private-label substitution and elasticity. A price rise with persistent volume loss is not automatically pricing power. |
| Retail, restaurants and distributors | Comparable sales, traffic/ticket split, inventory turns, unit-level returns, store maturity and closures | Lease-consistent operating value, normalized cash flow and earnings | Lease commitments, inventory aging, promotions and expansion capex. Distinguish company-owned and franchised units; low margins can coexist with strong capital turns. |
| Subscription software | Recurring revenue, gross and net retention, gross profit, customer acquisition payback, cash margin after compensation burden | Mature normalized earnings/cash; revenue multiples only with explicit margin and reinvestment assumptions | Stock compensation, dilution, capitalized commissions, customer prepayments and churn. Rule of 40 is a growth/profitability diagnostic, not a valuation; no universal gross-margin cutoff. |
| Platforms, payments and advertising | Transaction/user activity, net take rate, contribution per transaction, retention and monetization | Normalized net earnings or operating cash; multiples on recognized net revenue where appropriate | Gross transaction value is not revenue. Test network effects, customer multi-homing, fraud losses, regulation and traffic acquisition. Separate lenders from payment processors. |
| Semiconductors and hardware | Through-cycle margins, inventory, utilization, design wins, customer concentration and returns after research/capex | Cycle-normalized operating earnings and cash flow | Separate fabless designers, foundries, memory and equipment. Inspect capacity additions, technology transitions, research spending and asset obsolescence; peak utilization flatters returns. |
| Cyclical manufacturing, transport and shipping | Normalized volume/margins, utilization, replacement costs, industry capacity and trough liquidity | Mid-cycle earnings/cash and asset-value cross-checks | High fixed costs, order cancellations, leases and new supply. Do not capitalize peak earnings or assume a trough survives without funding. |
| Energy and mining | Commodity-price scenarios, unit costs, production decline, reserves, sustaining investment and returns through the cycle | Normalized cash flow and resource/asset value after royalties, taxes and closure costs | Separate producers, refiners, services and pipelines. EBITDA omits depletion and replacement economics; reserves require economic recoverability, not a universal life cutoff. |
| Regulated utilities | Rate-base growth, allowed versus earned equity returns, recovery lag and operating cash relative to obligations | Distributable equity cash/earnings, regulated asset value and sustainable dividends | Allowed returns are not guaranteed. Test capex funding, disallowed costs and equity issuance. Dividend yield versus bond yield alone does not determine value. |
| Telecom and infrastructure | Subscriber/unit economics, churn, utilization, pricing, returns after replacement and spectrum spending | Normalized cash after investment; lease-consistent enterprise multiples | Debt, renewal concessions, spectrum auctions and technology replacement. EBITDA can hide heavy ongoing investment. |
| Deposit-taking banks | Sustainable return on equity/tangible common equity, credit losses, loan quality, net interest margin, deposits and capital headroom | Price / tangible book alongside sustainable returns; normalized equity earnings or distributions | Funding concentration, uninsured deposits, securities losses and collateral access. Cheap book may conceal credit losses; CFO, corporate current ratios and enterprise EBITDA multiples are poor default measures. |
| Non-deposit lenders and specialty finance | Loan-vintage losses, credit quality, lending spread after losses/costs, equity returns and funding duration | Sustainable common-equity earnings/distributions and asset/NAV cross-checks | Warehouse funding, collateral haircuts, recourse and refinancing concentration. Do not import deposit-bank funding assumptions or ordinary corporate EBITDA multiples. |
| Property and casualty insurers | Combined ratio, reserve development, premium quality, investment returns and regulatory capital | Normalized equity earnings, book value relative to sustainable returns and distributable capital | Separate underwriting from investing. Check catastrophe accumulation, reinsurance limits and counterparty concentration. Reserve releases can flatter results; rapid growth may buy bad risks. Do not add float and the full investment portfolio without policyholder claims. |
| Life and health insurers | Life: spreads, guarantees, lapses and asset-liability matching. Health: medical-cost ratios, membership quality and pricing adequacy | Sustainable equity earnings and distributable capital; book-value cross-check | Stress rates, surrenders, claims and credit exposure. Do not apply the P&C combined ratio universally or compare different regulatory-capital formulas as identical. |
| Equity real estate investment trusts | Same-property net operating income, occupancy, rent collection, lease expiries, funds from operations per share | Adjusted funds from operations after recurring property/leasing costs; property net asset value | Reconcile FFO and company-defined AFFO. Neither is automatically distributable cash. Check straight-line rent, tenant quality, development spending and refinancing. Treat mortgage REITs as leveraged credit/asset-liability businesses instead. |
| Pharma and biotechnology | Sales by product, patent/exclusivity expiry, research productivity, pipeline probabilities and cash runway | Existing-product cash plus separately risk-adjusted pipeline value | Binary trials, reimbursement, concentration and replacement research. Do not assume pipeline approval or add pipeline value already inside the cash forecast. |
| Medical devices and care providers | Devices: installed base, procedure volume, recurring consumables. Providers: patient volumes, payer mix, reimbursement, staffing and collections | Normalized earnings/cash adjusted for recurring equipment and working capital | Product liability, recalls, labor costs and payer concentration. Separate device economics from hospital operations and health insurance. |
| Asset managers and exchanges | Managers: net flows, fee rates and recurring fee profit. Exchanges: volume, recurring data revenue and operating leverage | Normalized fee earnings/cash, with performance fees separated | Market appreciation is not organic inflow. Inspect fee pressure, client concentration and capital commitments. Do not apply deposit-bank metrics to a fee business by label alone. |
| Holding companies | Look-through earning power, investee value, parent liquidity and capital allocation | Sum of component equity values less parent claims, costs and tax leakage | Avoid double-counting subsidiaries, distributions and investments. Distinguish parent-only debt from claims already deducted at subsidiary level. |

## 4. Define and reconcile selected measures

Use these definitions where applicable; record any alternative explicitly.

| Measure | Working definition / limitation |
|:--|:--|
| Growth | Annual change or compound annual growth = (ending / beginning)^(1 / years) - 1 for positive comparable endpoints. Use absolute changes when loss-making or distorted. |
| ROIC | After-tax operating profit / average invested operating capital. Reconcile goodwill and leases. If long-lived research/customer investment is capitalized analytically, adjust earnings, unamortized capital and reinvestment together without changing cash spending/taxes. Otherwise disclose distortion. Tiny/negative capital makes interpretation unreliable. |
| ROE / ROTCE | Common earnings / average common equity or tangible common equity. Tangible equity excludes goodwill and relevant intangibles; specify earnings adjustments. Leverage can inflate returns. |
| Cash yield | Sustainable annual cash attributable to the chosen claimholders / corresponding equity or enterprise value. Label maintenance and growth investment assumptions. |
| Net retention | Ending recurring revenue from the starting customer cohort / its starting revenue, including expansion and churn but excluding new customers. Check the company's exact cohort definition. |
| Acquisition payback | Acquisition cost per new customer / monthly gross profit contribution per customer. Align periods and include attributable selling costs; use observed retention, not assumed infinite life. |
| P&C combined ratio | Ratio for losses and loss-adjustment expenses plus underwriting-expense ratio, using disclosed premium denominators. Below 100% indicates underwriting profit, not total investment profitability. |
| Property FFO / AFFO | Reconcile reported profit to property FFO adjustments, then recurring spending and other cash adjustments. AFFO definitions differ; inspect rather than trust the label. |
| Enterprise value | Common-equity market value plus relevant debt, preferred and minority claims, less eligible non-operating cash/assets; align every adjustment with the earnings perimeter. |

## 5. Apply an alternative valuation when needed

Select only a method that fits the assets, claims and evidence. Fix valuation date, ownership perimeter, currency and units. For discounted estimates here use the nominal 10% required-return convention: `PV(x_t) = x_t / 1.10^t`; x_t is a signed amount at time t in years from that date, and n is the last forecast time. Net common distributions mean dividends plus repurchases less common equity funding. Keep nominal forecasts consistent with the convention, not measured financing cost. Date receipts, investment, liabilities and terminal value; do not discount present values twice.

| Method / use | Procedure and essential checks |
|:--|:--|
| Asset / net asset value (NAV) | Choose going-concern asset values or liquidation recoveries; do not mix their assumptions. Estimate each asset at a supportable value, allowing for recoverability, encumbrances, sale timing, taxes and disposal/wind-down costs. Common NAV = asset values - senior/minority/other claims - uncounted costs, all on a common date. Show book-to-economic adjustments. Test lower recoveries and longer realization; book or replacement cost is not a guaranteed floor. |
| Sum of the parts | Value each material segment/holding with an appropriate method. Establish its full common-equity value after its own senior claims, then apply effective ownership; do not also deduct that same minority interest. Sum attributable values plus uncounted parent assets, less parent-only claims, tax leakage and present value of unallocated overhead. Eliminate intercompany items; deduct subsidiary debt or costs only once. Identify market-priced components. Support any further holding-company discount rather than applying one automatically. |
| Finite-life or uneven cash flows | Schedule operating receipts, investment and financing appropriate to the chosen claimholders through exhaustion, expiry or recovery. Include negative/zero years, later closure/handback costs and defensible salvage proceeds. Sum their present values; add no perpetual continuation without supported renewal/replacement economics and its investment. Reconcile enterprise-to-equity claims only if the schedule is before financing. |
| Financial-company equity cash | Forecast common distributions supported by normalized earnings, asset quality, asset growth and required capital. Include necessary external equity funding as negative net distributions; do not subtract deposits as ordinary corporate debt. Value = present value of net common distributions plus terminal common-equity value. Use a perpetuity only for sustainable positive distributable cash: terminal value at n = next-period cash / (0.10 - g), with supported constant -1 < g < 0.10 and retained capital funding that growth. Otherwise use an explicit runoff or bounded schedule. |
| Financial-company excess returns | Use reconciled common book value B and common earnings E on one consistent basis. Check `B_t = B_(t-1) + E_t - net common distributions`; reconcile other comprehensive income and direct equity changes before applying it. Residual income `RI_t = E_t - 0.10 * B_(t-1)`. Common value = `B_0 + SUM(PV(RI_t)) + PV(terminal common-equity value - B_n)`. Support continuation/fade; terminal excess is zero only when future excess returns disappear under the stated assumptions. Do not add full terminal equity or the investment portfolio again. |

Reconcile alternative estimates, not add valuations of the same assets and their earnings. Distinguish fundamental estimates, market-based comparisons and liquidation assumptions. Include existing equity claims once before dividing by the relevant common shares once. Show any negative residual separately, not as a negative tradable share price. If material ownership, recoveries, funding or accounting bridges remain unresolved, report the limitation rather than manufacture a value.

## 6. Check the selection before calculating

MUST verify business/method fit, definitions, dated periods, cycle normalization, ownership and claims, funding needs and main failure risks. Reproduce any valuation with a second calculation and check no asset, income stream or claim is counted twice. **PASS:** the selected measures and method support the stated analysis. **HALT** unsupported numbers or incompatible methods; an explicit Not assessable finding with its missing inputs is valid.

Keep the working selection to a few measures, one principal method and a useful cross-check where available. This file is a selection and method guide, not a sector score or company verdict.
