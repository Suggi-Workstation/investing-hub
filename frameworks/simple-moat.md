# Simple Moat Analysis

## 1. Define the business being assessed

Record company, date, customer, product, geography and main competitors. Separate materially different segments. Review 5-10 years where available, including weak conditions. Compare like-for-like competitors, not unrelated high-margin businesses.

Identify why customers choose this business and who captures the benefit. Distinguish structural advantage from execution, popularity, market share or temporary shortage. Corroborate decisive claims with independent customer, competitor or industry evidence. A primary source establishes origin, not neutrality; repeated versions of one claim are not independent observations.

## 2. Answer the moat questions

For each mechanism below, record **Present / Absent / Unknown**, a measured example, the strongest contrary evidence and confidence. Do not award extra points merely for naming several overlapping mechanisms.

| Mechanism | Questions that MUST be answered |
|:--|:--|
| Switching costs | What money, time, risk or disruption does changing supplier impose? Do retention, renewal behavior or customer testimony confirm it? Can customers easily use multiple suppliers? |
| Network effects | Does an additional participant improve value for other participants? Is the effect local or global? Can users use competing networks at once or transact outside the platform? |
| Brand, patents or licenses | Does the asset produce willingness to pay, preferential access or protected economics? When can patents, licenses, regulation or customer preferences undermine it? |
| Structural cost advantage | What keeps unit costs below competitors after maintenance and reinvestment? Can a funded rival reproduce the process, resource access, distribution or scale? |
| Efficient scale | Is demand too limited to support another profitable entrant? Are capacity, regulation and competitor economics consistent with that claim? |
| Scale economies shared | Are scale savings passed to customers, improving value and retention, then increasing volume and further lowering costs? Is there evidence of this loop without persistent loss-making subsidies? |

## 3. Test Porter's competitive forces

Answer each question in the final force table. Rate pressure **Low / Medium / High / Unknown**; High always means more pressure on profits. Identify the strongest force and whether the company's advantage actually neutralizes it. Do not average industry pressure into a company moat score.

## 4. Verify the economics and durability

- Check multi-year returns on capital, cash generation after necessary reinvestment, pricing versus volume, retention and relevant unit costs. Explain the mechanism behind the result.
- Where meaningful, calculate return on invested capital as after-tax operating profit divided by average invested operating capital. Compare with a defensible capital-cost estimate and peers. Identify distortions from goodwill, expensed research or a tiny/negative capital base; use suitable sector evidence instead of forcing the ratio.
- Separate cyclical windfalls, leverage and underinvestment from durable economics. Low margins alone do not disprove a low-cost, fast-turnover or customer-savings model.
- Ask: if a well-funded competitor attacked, how would it take the customers? What would destroy the advantage? Would ordinary competent management preserve it?
- Distinguish protection of existing economics from opportunities to expand. A durable niche can have a strong moat without a large reinvestment runway; do not score growth potential as moat strength.
- Name the nearest credible threat, likely timing, and an observable warning trigger. Check capacity additions, substitutes, technology, concentration, regulation and dependence on one patent, supplier or channel. State whether evidence indicates widening, stable or narrowing protection.
- Ask who benefits or bears material harm, and what credible customer, employee, supplier or regulatory response could undermine the economics. Record ethical concerns separately from demonstrated moat erosion; neither profitability nor low prices prove ethical conduct.

## 5. Score and classify

Use this coarse house rubric, not an investor-attributed formula. Score 1-5; use 2 or 4 for intermediate evidence and N/A for unsupported dimensions. Do not calculate an overall mean or average mechanism counts or industry pressure into the ratings.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Protection | Easily copied or bypassed | Meaningful but limited defense | Demonstrated, difficult-to-replicate defense |
| Economic proof | No sustained advantage after reinvestment | Mixed or moderately superior economics | Persistent superior economics through adverse conditions |
| Durability | Credible near-term erosion | Defensible, with material vulnerabilities | Strong defenses against identified long-term threats |
| Trend | Demonstrated erosion | Broadly stable | Measurable, sustained strengthening |

Classify in order: **Unclear** if conclusion-critical evidence is missing or materially contradictory; **None** if adequate evidence establishes no durable economic advantage; otherwise **Wide** when protection, economic proof and durability each score at least 4, **Narrow** when each is at least 3, and **Weak** for an evidenced durable advantage below those standards. Keep trend separate. High-confidence None is a valid conclusion.

Confidence: **High** for consistent multi-year primary and independent evidence; **Medium** for partial corroboration; **Low** for sparse, contradictory or promotional evidence. High scores MUST have primary support. Overall confidence cannot exceed the weakest conclusion-critical premise; quantity of documents does not resolve a contradiction.

For a full assessment, **PASS** requires answered mechanisms and forces, corroborated economics, checked calculations, consistent classification and a threat/trigger. A supported None or Unclear may end early with decisive evidence, limitations and unassessed fields N/A; identify what would change the finding. **HALT** unsupported ratings, not defensible negative or uncertain results. A low price MUST NOT improve the moat rating.
