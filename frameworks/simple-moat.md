# Simple Moat Analysis

## 1. Establish the scope

Record the company, date, paying customers, market and main competitors. Assess materially different businesses separately. Review several years, including a difficult period where available.

## 2. Examine competitive pressure

Assess rivalry, new entrants, supplier power, buyer power and substitutes. Mark each **Low / Medium / High** pressure, or **Unknown** if evidence is missing. Add one supporting fact. Identify the strongest threat; do not average these labels.

## 3. Test the advantage

Identify what competitors cannot easily copy: costly customer switching, networks that improve with participation, brand or legal protection, structural cost advantages, or a market too small for additional profitable competitors.

Check whether that protection produces sustained cash generation and attractive returns after reinvestment. Compare with competitors. Do not treat size, popularity, high margins or brilliant management alone as a moat.

Ask what could destroy the advantage and whether it survives a management change. Record whether it is strengthening, stable or weakening.

## 4. Score and check

Score the categories below: **1 absent/poor, 2 weak, 3 moderate, 4 strong, 5 exceptional**. Use evidence, not adjectives; mark missing evidence **N/A**.

Overall = sum of category scores / number of categories, rounded to one decimal. If any category is N/A, overall is N/A. Keep competitive-pressure labels separate from scores; higher scores mean stronger protection.

**PASS:** both tables complete, ratings supported, average correct, and the main threat stated. Confirm a moat only when both a protective mechanism and durable economic evidence exist. **HALT** unsupported conclusions. Keep share price and valuation outside this assessment.
