# Simple Management Analysis

## 1. Establish the record

Record the company, date, key managers and their tenure. Review annual reports, ownership/pay disclosures and capital decisions over several years. Do not credit current managers with a predecessor's entire record.

## 2. Check integrity first

Check accounting reliability, candor during setbacks, related-party dealings and treatment of shareholders. Distinguish allegations from established facts; a share sale alone does not prove dishonesty.

Established serious dishonesty: mark **AVOID**, overriding every score. An unresolved material reliability concern: mark **INVESTIGATE**, with no overall numerical score.

## 3. Assess decisions and incentives

Trace cash into reinvestment, acquisitions, debt repayment, dividends and buybacks. Judge durable per-share value and financial resilience, not company size. Evaluate buybacks against estimated value at the time; reconcile actual dilution rather than subtracting compensation expense from repurchase spending.

Check whether ownership and pay reward long-term shareholder outcomes. Separate economic ownership from voting control. Assess board oversight, minority protection and succession.

Compare at least two dated decisions or commitments with outcomes. Include a setback where available and assess the response. Separate management's contribution from favorable industry conditions.

## 4. Score and check

Score each category: **1 poor, 2 weak, 3 adequate, 4 strong, 5 exceptional**. Give one supporting fact and one concern. Missing evidence is **N/A**, not a neutral score.

If integrity permits scoring, overall = sum of category scores / number of categories, rounded to one decimal. If any category is N/A, overall is N/A; do not average only the available scores.

**PASS:** every category addressed, evidence supports ratings, arithmetic checked, and integrity concerns remain visible. **HALT** unsupported scores. A good average never cancels dishonesty. Keep the investment-price decision separate.
