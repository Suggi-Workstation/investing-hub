# Alibaba -- Owner Earnings Far Below Reported Profit, and a Price Above Base Value

Alibaba runs the Taobao and Tmall marketplaces and the cloud business with the largest share of China's market, and it is spending more on AI data centres than its operations bring in.[1][10]
Free cash flow was an outflow of RMB46,609 million in fiscal 2026, and adjusted EBITA fell from RMB173,065 million to RMB76,416 million.[1]
The company attributes the fall in e-commerce profit primarily to its investment in quick commerce, user experiences and technology, made during a price war across China's e-commerce sector.[1][11]
Normalized owner earnings are estimated here at about RMB4.65 per ordinary share, 5.2% of the share price, against about RMB1.85 when the current AI losses are counted in full (derived).
**Thesis status:** Thesis not supported at the current price. **Confidence:** Low. **Valuation status:** Completed as an earnings proxy; Base value HK$86 per ordinary share against a HK$104.60 price.

**Identity and basis:** Alibaba Group Holding Limited (Cayman Islands); ordinary shares 9988 on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange and American depositary shares BABA on the NYSE; each ADS represents eight Shares.[1]
One class of shares; 19,901,959,514 issued shares at the close of October 2026, with no treasury shares.[5]
Reviewed 2026-11-10 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
US GAAP, consolidated including variable interest entities, fiscal year ending March 31; RMB millions unless stated; values per ordinary share in Hong Kong dollars.
**History covered:** fiscal 2024 to fiscal 2026 and the quarter ended June 30, 2026; results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 are due on November 19, 2026.[1][2][6]

**Summary:** Alibaba is a profitable marketplace business paying for two large bets: subsidies to defend its shoppers and data centres to rent out AI computing.
The marketplaces need little capital; the cloud needs more than the whole group earns.
Reported profit, free cash flow and owner earnings therefore give three different pictures, and only the last separates what the business earns from what it chooses to invest.
The accounting trap is fiscal 2026 net income, most of which came from investment gains (see Financial Health).

| Network effects | Present, weakened | Shoppers come for the merchants and merchants pay to reach the shoppers.[1] | Customer management revenue decreased by 7% in the June 2026 quarter while rivals subsidized.[2][11] | Medium |

**Summary:** The marketplaces still have a network effect, but it now has to be defended with subsidies.
The cloud has the larger share of its market and real switching costs, yet earns a thin margin.
Neither business currently shows protected high returns on the capital it is taking in.

## Valuation

**Coverage: Assessed.** Valuation date 2026-11-10; simple DCF at a 10% required return on normalized owner earnings; RMB millions, converted at HK$1.17517 per RMB (derived from [13]); 19,901.96 million shares.[5]
**Valuation type: earnings proxy.** Owner earnings here are adjusted EBITA of the ongoing businesses less share-based compensation and tax, assuming the capital spending needed to hold their position equals depreciation; free cash flow is negative, so the estimate is not reconciled to distributable cash.
Input line (Base): Alibaba, 2026-11-10; V0 92,625; A 393,505; S 19,901.96; P HK$104.60 (derived).
Owner earnings per ordinary share: about RMB4.65, or HK$5.47, a 5.2% yield on the price and about 7.5% on the enterprise value left after the balance sheet (derived).
Counting the AI losses in full, non-GAAP net income less share-based compensation over the last four quarters was 36,915, about RMB1.85 per share, a 2.1% yield (derived from [1][2]).

**Summary:** The three cases differ mainly in e-commerce profit, which has ranged from about 105,000 to 178,000 a year within two years (derived from [1][2]).
A adds net cash of 207,975, the placing proceeds and the investments at a discount, and subtracts minority interests of 64,856 and the AI Labs losses for the years shown (derived from [2]).
The Bear case also writes off half of the roughly RMB190 billion still to be spent under the AI plan (assumption).
New cloud capital is assumed to earn the required return in the Base case, so cloud growth adds nothing beyond today's cloud profit.

**Summary:** The range is wide because the two decisive inputs are both unsettled: what the marketplaces earn once the price war ends, and what the AI capital returns.
E-commerce profit 10% higher or lower moves the Base value by about HK$6.70 a share; ten points on the investment discount by about HK$2.70; a 20% tax rate adds about HK$4.90; with no growth the Base value is about HK$78 (derived).
At HK$104.60 the price requires normalized owner earnings of about 120,265, 30% above Base, which corresponds to e-commerce adjusted EBITA of about 169,000 a year with AI capital earning only its cost (derived).
The price therefore assumes that e-commerce profit returns most of the way to its fiscal 2025 level, or that the cloud earns well above its cost of capital.
Per ADS the values are about US$47, US$88 and US$147 (derived from [13]).
Confidence is Low: the arithmetic reproduces, but one quarter of recovered e-commerce profit is thin evidence for the central case.

**Summary:** The weakest assumption is the normal level of e-commerce profit, which moved by more than two-fifths in a single year.
The next evidence is the September quarter: e-commerce adjusted EBITA near or above the June quarter's level would support the Base case, and a fall back toward fiscal 2026's average would point to the Bear case.
Evidence of cloud returns above cost, such as rising margins while spending slows, would move the valuation toward the Bull case.

### Final Verdict

Alibaba's marketplaces still earn large sums, but their moat is being defended with subsidies, managers control the board with a small stake, and the company is spending more than it earns on AI capacity whose return is unproven.[1][2]
At HK$104.60 per ordinary share (Hong Kong close, November 9, 2026) the price stands against Bear/Base/Bull estimated values of HK$46/HK$86/HK$144, 22% above the Base value (derived from [12]).[12]
These are earnings-proxy values, not intrinsic values from a cash DCF, so no margin of safety is claimed.
The judgment is overvalued relative to Base, with Low confidence: the price needs e-commerce profit close to its level before the price war, or cloud returns well above cost.
The decisive risk is that subsidies and AI spending both stay high while neither earns its cost.
