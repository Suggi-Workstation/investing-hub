# Alibaba -- Owner Earnings Far Below Reported Profit, and a Price Now Below Base Value

Alibaba runs the Taobao and Tmall marketplaces and the cloud business with the largest share of China's market, and it is spending more on AI data centres than its operations bring in.[1][10]
Free cash flow was an outflow of RMB34,472 million in the quarter ended September 30, 2026, as capital expenditures of RMB50,477 million exceeded net cash provided by operating activities of RMB15,690 million.[14]
Alibaba E-commerce Group adjusted EBITA rose to RMB38,851 million from RMB11,336 million a year earlier, primarily driven by a significant loss reduction in the China Quick Commerce business.[14]
Normalized owner earnings are estimated here at about RMB5.46 per ordinary share, 7.8% of the share price, against about RMB2.34 when the current AI losses are counted in full (derived).
**Thesis status:** Investigate. **Confidence:** Low. **Valuation status:** Completed as an earnings proxy; Base value HK$99 per ordinary share against a HK$82.20 price.

**Identity and basis:** Alibaba Group Holding Limited (Cayman Islands); ordinary shares 9988 on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange and American depositary shares BABA on the NYSE; each ADS represents eight Shares.[1]
One class of shares; 19,901,959,514 issued shares at the close of October 2026, with no treasury shares.[5]
Reviewed 2026-11-25 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
US GAAP, consolidated including variable interest entities, fiscal year ending March 31; RMB millions unless stated; values per ordinary share in Hong Kong dollars.
**History covered:** fiscal 2024 to fiscal 2026 and the quarters ended June 30 and September 30, 2026; the September quarter results came out on November 19, 2026, the date the board had set.[1][2][6][14]

**Summary:** Alibaba is a profitable marketplace business paying for two large bets: subsidies to defend its shoppers and data centres to rent out AI computing.
The marketplaces need little capital; the cloud needs more than the whole group earns.
Reported profit, free cash flow and owner earnings therefore give three different pictures, and only the last separates what the business earns from what it chooses to invest.
The accounting trap is reported net income, most of which in fiscal 2026 and in the September 2026 quarter came from investment gains (see Financial Health).

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed.** Perimeter: the China marketplaces and the China cloud business, judged separately.

| Mechanism | Present / Absent / Unknown | Answer / evidence | Contrary evidence | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|

| Network effects | Present, weakened | Shoppers come for the merchants and merchants pay to reach the shoppers.[1] | Customer management revenue decreased by 5% in the September 2026 quarter, or grew 1% like-for-like.[14] | Medium |
| Brand, patents or licenses | Present, limited | Taobao and Tmall are established marketplace brands.[1] | Brand has not stopped the price war.[11] | Low |
| Structural cost advantage | Unknown | Scale in data centres and own chips. | Cloud adjusted EBITA is 14.5% of revenue, which shows no clear cost advantage yet (derived from [14]). | Low |
| Efficient scale | Absent | Several funded rivals in both markets.[10][11] | None needed. | Medium |
| Scale economies shared | Unknown | A new business development program is recorded as contra revenue: a defence, not a cost saving passed on.[14] | E-commerce adjusted EBITA fell while the subsidies ran (see Management).[1] | Low |

**Summary:** The marketplaces still have a network effect, but it now has to be defended with subsidies.
The cloud has the larger share of its market and real switching costs, and its margin is rising while compute is scarce.
Neither business yet shows protected high returns on the capital it is taking in.

**Summary:** Rivalry is still the decisive force in commerce, though the main rivals are now cutting their subsidies too.
The cloud faces supplier pressure from chip prices and from US export rules, the same scarcity that lets it raise prices.
Nothing in the evidence yet shows the marketplace defence holding without subsidies through a weak consumer quarter.

## Valuation

**Coverage: Assessed.** Valuation date 2026-11-25; simple DCF at a 10% required return on normalized owner earnings; RMB millions, converted at HK$1.17666 per RMB (derived from [13]); 19,901.96 million shares.[5]
**Valuation type: earnings proxy.** Owner earnings here are adjusted EBITA of the ongoing businesses less share-based compensation and tax, assuming the capital spending needed to hold their position equals depreciation; free cash flow is negative, so the estimate is not reconciled to distributable cash.
Input line (Base): Alibaba, 2026-11-25; V0 108,764; A 427,082; S 19,901.96; P HK$82.20 (derived).
Owner earnings per ordinary share: about RMB5.46, or HK$6.43, a 7.8% yield on the price and about 12.7% on the enterprise value left after the balance sheet (derived).
Counting the AI losses in full, non-GAAP net income less share-based compensation over the last four quarters was 46,490, about RMB2.34 per share, a 3.3% yield (derived from [1][2][14]).

**Summary:** The weakest assumption is still the normal level of e-commerce profit, now joined by the return on AI capital under US export rules.
The price no longer asks for a recovery: it needs e-commerce profit below the last four quarters with AI capital at its cost, but the estimate is an earnings proxy, so the gap to price is not a margin of safety.
E-commerce adjusted EBITA holding near 38,000 a quarter through the December quarter would support the Base case; a slide toward 27,800 a quarter would mean the price was right (derived).
A second share issue before cash flow turns positive would move the valuation toward the Bear case.

### Final Verdict

Alibaba's marketplaces earn more again as quick-commerce losses narrow, but their moat is defended with subsidies, managers control the board with a small stake, and the company spends more than it earns on AI capacity whose return is unproven and whose supply US export rules now restrict.[14][15][17]
At HK$82.20 per ordinary share (Hong Kong close, November 24, 2026) the price stands against Bear/Base/Bull estimated values of HK$53/HK$99/HK$153, 17% below the Base value (derived from [12]).[12]
These are earnings-proxy values, not intrinsic values from a cash DCF, so no margin of safety is claimed.
The judgment is undervalued relative to Base, with Low confidence: the price needs e-commerce adjusted EBITA of only about 111,000 a year with AI capital at its cost, below what the last four quarters produced.
The decisive risk is that AI spending stays high, earns below its cost under export controls and is funded with more shares while subsidies return.
