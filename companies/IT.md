# Gartner -- A Subscription Research Franchise Whose Growth Stalled, Priced for Decline

Gartner sells research subscriptions, conferences and consulting to technology and business executives, and revenue grew from $2,444,540 thousand in 2016 to $6,497,226 thousand in 2025.[1][6]
Contract value, the annual value of subscriptions, grew 8% in 2024 but only 1.7% in the year to June 2026, and the 10-K warns that AI is expected to bring more competition.[1][2][4]
Management responded with buybacks: Gartner repurchased $2 billion of stock in 2025 and $547 million in the second quarter of 2026, partly funded with new bonds.[2][3]
**Thesis status:** Thesis supported with a modest margin. **Confidence:** Low. **Valuation status:** Completed; Base value $266 per share against a $207.31 price.

**Identity and basis:** Gartner, Inc. (Delaware), common stock IT on the NYSE; one class; 63,148,976 shares outstanding at July 31, 2026.[5]

The core measure is contract value, and its growth fell from +8% YoY at end-2024 to +1.7% YoY in Q2 2026.[2][4]
The market's fear is that AI tools give executives the same answers more cheaply.[1]

**Summary:** Gartner's moat is a standard of reference: vendors want to rank well in its reports and buyers use them to choose vendors.[1]
That is durable while executives value human analysts, and the 10-K expects more competition from AI.[1]
The stall in contract value growth is the first evidence that the moat is under pressure.[2][4]

**Summary:** Substitution is the decisive force, exactly as for Adobe and Intuit: the threat is a cheaper answer, not a direct rival.[1]
Against that, Global Business Sales contract value still grew 3.3% in Q2 2026.[2]
The evidence cannot yet separate cyclical budget pressure from structural substitution.

### Final Verdict

Gartner is a high-margin subscription business with a narrow and narrowing moat, adequate owner-operator management and resilient finances.[1][2][5]
At the NYSE close of $207.31 on 2026-10-15, Bear / Base / Bull cash-DCF values of $134 / $266 / $384 put the price 21.9% below Base and 54.4% above Bear (derived from the Valuation table; price from [8]).
My judgment is undervalued against Base, with Low confidence: the price already discounts decline, but contract value growth has nearly stopped.
Contract value growth falling below zero would invalidate the Base case; a price near $186 would give a 30% margin.
