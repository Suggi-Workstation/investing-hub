# Fair Isaac -- A Toll Road Whose Busiest Lane Is Now Contested by Regulators

Fair Isaac sells the FICO Score, the standard measure of US consumer credit risk, and decision software to banks.[1]
Scores revenue grew from 241,059 thousand in fiscal 2016 to 1,168,575 thousand in fiscal 2025, mostly through price, and the business earns operating margins above 40%.[1][7]
In 2026 the mortgage regulator opened Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans to VantageScore, a rival owned by FICO's own distributors, and aligned the fees for both scores.[8][9]
**Thesis status:** Thesis not supported at the current price. **Confidence:** Low. **Valuation status:** Completed; Base value $700 per share against a $769.93 price.

**Identity and basis:** Fair Isaac Corporation (Delaware), common stock FICO on the NYSE; one class; 21,597,635 shares outstanding on July 17, 2026.[2]
Reviewed 2026-10-16 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
US GAAP consolidated statements; fiscal year ends September 30; amounts in USD millions unless stated in thousands, per share or in percent.[1]
**History covered:** fiscal 2016 and fiscal 2023-2025 annual statements and the nine months to June 30, 2026.[1][2][7]

It needs almost no capital, so owner cash follows revenue and price closely (derived).[1]
Most recent growth came from mortgage score prices, which is exactly the lane regulators reopened to competition in 2026.[2][8]

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed.** Perimeter: Scores, 59% of fiscal 2025 revenue (derived), where nearly all the profit is earned.[1]

| Mechanism | Present / Absent / Unknown | Answer / evidence | Contrary evidence | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Switching costs | Present | Lenders' models, investor reporting and capital rules are built on Classic FICO.[10] | Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac now disclose a VantageScore 4.0 score on all securitized mortgages.[9] | High |
| Network effects | Present | A common score lets lenders, investors and insurers price the same risk the same way.[1] | Regulators now accept two scores.[8] | Medium |
| Brand, patents or licenses | Present | The FICO Score is the standard measure of consumer credit risk in the US.[1] | VantageScore is owned by the three bureaus that distribute FICO.[9] | High |
| Structural cost advantage | Absent | Scores are algorithms run on bureau data. | None. | High |
| Efficient scale | Present | One standard serves the whole market. | A regulator can create a second standard. | Medium |
| Scale economies shared | Absent | Prices have risen, not fallen. | None. | High |

**Summary:** FICO's moat is a standard: everyone uses the score because everyone else does, and the capital rules were built on it.[1][10]
That is among the strongest moats in business, but a standard set partly by government buyers can be changed by government.[8]
In the four months from May 1, 2026, VantageScore 4.0 was used for approximately 9% of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac mortgages.[9]

| Force / question | Pressure | Evidence-based answer | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Rivalry: can rivals compete away profit through price or capacity? | Medium | VantageScore competes in mortgages after FHFA aligned upfront fees across both models on September 30, 2026.[8] | Medium |
| Entry: what prevents a funded newcomer from winning customers? | Low | Validation by regulators and decades of performance data.[1] | High |
| Suppliers: can essential providers capture the margin? | High | FICO depends on bureau data and bureau distribution; the bureaus own VantageScore.[1][9] | High |
| Buyers: can concentrated or price-sensitive customers force concessions? | High | Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and their regulator set mortgage score policy.[8] | High |
| Substitutes: can another solution meet the need more cheaply or better? | Medium | Rocket Mortgage and United Wholesale Mortgage have adopted VantageScore 4.0.[9] | Medium |

**Summary:** Two forces press at once: the suppliers of data and distribution own the rival, and the largest buyer's regulator has chosen competition.[8][9]
Outside mortgages, in cards, auto and account management, nothing in the evidence shows the standard weakening.[1]
The mortgage lane matters because it drove the B2B price gains of 2025-2026.[2][3]

**Summary:** The business generates cash reliably, but the balance sheet was deliberately levered to buy back stock: debt rose from 3,055,691 to 5,582,389 thousand in nine months.[2]

That is manageable while credit markets are open, but it turns a regulatory setback into a financing question.

**Summary:** The weakest assumption in any bull case is that FICO can keep raising mortgage prices while its regulator promotes a rival owned by its own distributors.[8][9]
Buffett's four filters: understandable, superb economics, able but aggressive management, and a price that is not low enough for the new risk.
The next evidence is fiscal 2027 guidance in November 2026 and the share of GSE loans scored by VantageScore.

### Final Verdict

FICO is a superb business with a wide but narrowing moat, adequate management that levered the balance sheet to buy back stock above value, and finances on Watch.[1][2]
At the NYSE close of $769.93 on 2026-10-15, Bear / Base / Bull cash-DCF values of $170 / $700 / $1,353 put the price 10.1% above Base and 43.1% below Bull (derived from the Valuation table; price from [11]).
My judgment is fairly valued to slightly overvalued relative to Base, with Low confidence: the sell-off priced in some regulatory risk but not a margin of safety.
A sustained VantageScore share gain in GSE mortgages would invalidate the Base case; a price near $490 would give a 30% margin.
