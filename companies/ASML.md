# ASML -- The Only EUV Maker, Priced as if a Boom Year's Margin Were Normal

ASML makes the lithography machines that print circuit patterns on chips, and it is currently the world's only manufacturer of EUV lithography systems.[1]
It expects 2026 total net sales between EUR 44 billion and EUR 45 billion with a gross margin around 56%, after guiding in January to between EUR 34 billion and EUR 39 billion.[2][5]
Normalized owner earnings are about EUR 12,400 million a year, EUR 32.33 a share and 2.4% of the share price; at the 2026 level of profit they are about EUR 41 a share (derived).
**Thesis status:** Thesis not supported at the current price. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed as a market-multiple hybrid; Base value EUR 1,012 per share against a EUR 1,343.80 price.

**Identity and basis:** ASML Holding N.V. (Netherlands); ordinary shares ASML on Euronext Amsterdam and Nasdaq; 385,417,665 Ordinary Shares outstanding at the end of 2025.[1]
Reviewed 2026-11-10 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
US GAAP, consolidated, fiscal year ending December 31; EUR millions unless stated; values per ordinary share in euros.
**History covered:** fiscal 2015, fiscal 2023 to 2025 and the first nine months of 2026.[1][3][8]

## Business and Method

**Coverage: Assessed.**

| Question | Assessment / evidence |
|:--|:--|
| Who pays, for what, and why do customers return? | Chipmakers buy the machines and then pay to service and upgrade them. In 2025 net system sales were 24,474.3 and net service and field option sales 8,193.0.[1] |
| Segments, geography and principal competitors | One business. The largest customer was 23.9% of total net sales in 2025 and the two largest 38.0%; customers in China represented 29.1%.[1] No other company makes EUV systems; older DUV machines have competitors.[1][9] |
| Main revenue, margin and cash-generation drivers | Customers' spending on new chip factories, and the mix of EUV machines and upgrades. Gross margin was 52.8% in 2025 and 57.5% in the third quarter of 2026.[1][2] |
| Capital required and who bears financing risk | Research is the main investment and is expensed. Customers pay in advance: net contract liabilities were EUR 18.9 billion as of December 31, 2025, mainly driven by down payments.[1] |
| Sector-specific measures and historical comparison | Total net sales were 27,558.5, 28,262.9 and 32,667.3 in 2023, 2024 and 2025, and net income 7,839.0, 7,571.6 and 9,609.4.[1] |
| Circle of competence and decisive limitations | The business is understandable; the cycle is not predictable. Orders depend on a few customers' capital budgets and on export rules set by governments.[1][10] |
| Principal valuation method / useful cross-check | Simple DCF on normalized owner earnings. Cross-checks: free cash flow against net income over several years, and the profit and growth the price requires. |

**Summary:** ASML sells the one machine that the most advanced chips cannot be made without, to a handful of buyers.
Those buyers pay in advance and keep paying for service and upgrades.
Sales follow the building of new chip capacity, so a good year and a normal year are far apart.
The accounting point in its favour is that research and share-based pay are already charged against profit.

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed.**

| Mechanism | Present / Absent / Unknown | Answer / evidence | Contrary evidence | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Switching costs | Present | Customers keep paying for the machines they own: net service and field option sales rose 26.2% in 2025.[1] | Buyers are few and large. | Medium |
| Network effects | Absent | None. | None. | High |
| Brand, patents or licenses | Present | The only manufacturer of EUV lithography systems, with an exclusive arrangement with Carl Zeiss SMT, its sole supplier of critical optics.[1] | Output is limited by that supplier's capacity.[1] | High |
| Structural cost advantage | Unknown | Not shown separately from the technology lead. | None found. | Low |
| Efficient scale | Present | Customers paid to create the one supplier: Intel, Samsung and TSMC acquired 23% of ASML's shares and agreed to fund EUR 1.38 billion of its R&D projects from 2013 through 2017.[8] | China has begun mass producing domestically developed immersion DUV machines.[9] | Medium |
| Scale economies shared | Unknown | No evidence. | None found. | Low |

**Summary:** The protection is a technology that took customers' own money and many years to build, and that nobody else has reproduced.
It is strongest in EUV and weaker in the older DUV machines, where a state-backed Chinese rival has started production.
The supplier of optics is a single point of dependence.

**Summary:** Competitive pressure is low where the profit is made.
The forces that can hurt ASML are its customers' budgets and governments' export decisions, not a rival's price.
Those are risks to earnings in a given year more than to the position itself.

## Valuation

**Coverage: Assessed.** Valuation date 2026-11-10; simple DCF at a 10% required return; EUR millions.
**Valuation type: market-multiple hybrid.** Owner earnings are net income, which already bears research and share-based pay, and they reconcile to cash: free cash flow after all capital spending was 94% of net income from 2023 to September 2026 (derived from [1][3]).
The exit multiple is a judgment, so these are not intrinsic values from a cash DCF.
Input line: ASML, 2026-11-10; owner earnings to ordinary shareholders; V0 12,400; A 10,772.1; S 383.5 million; P EUR 1,343.80 (derived).
V0 is 2026 guided sales of 44,500 at the 2023 to 2025 net margin of 28.3%, less after-tax interest income of about 154 on the cash counted in A, rounded (derived from [1][2][3]).
The 2026 net margin is about 35.5%, with gross margin at a record; V0 treats the difference as a boom (assumption; derived).
A is cash and short-term investments less long-term debt; S is the third quarter's diluted weighted average (derived from [3]).
Owner earnings per share: EUR 32.33, a 2.4% yield on the price and about 2.5% on enterprise value; about EUR 41 at the 2026 level of profit, a 3.1% yield (derived).

| Case | g1 / g2 / X | PV years 1-10 | PV terminal | Value/share (market-multiple hybrid) | Price at 30% discount | Price at 50% discount |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Base | 12% / 8% / 20 | 129,698 | 247,590 | 1,012 | 708 | 506 |
| Bull | 18% / 12% / 25 | 169,907 | 481,876 | 1,728 | 1,209 | 864 |
| Bear | 3% / 3% / 14 | 87,919 | 89,949 | 492 | 344 | 246 |

**Summary:** The Base path takes owner earnings from 12,400 to about 32,100 in ten years, which is about 7.4% a year from the 2026 level of profit (derived).
It sits near the middle of the company's 2030 scenarios; the Bull case needs the top of them and more, and the Bear case leaves profit in ten years about where it is in 2026 (assumption).
With no growth and the same exit multiple the value is about EUR 476 a share (derived).
An exit at 16 times instead of 20 lowers the Base value by about EUR 129 a share, and each 10% on owner earnings moves it by about EUR 98 (derived).
The terminal value is 64% of the Base value, and 20 times implies continuing growth of about 4.8% a year after the tenth year (derived).
At EUR 1,343.80 the price requires owner earnings of about 16,600 at Base growth and exit, 34% above V0 and about 6% above the 2026 level of profit (derived).
Treating 2026 profit as the normal level gives about EUR 1,269 a share, so that one generous assumption nearly bridges value and price (derived).
Put as growth, the price implies owner earnings rising about 14.0% a year for ten years from V0, or about 11.2% a year from the 2026 level (derived).
Confidence is Medium: the business facts are firm, and the decisive input is whether a margin earned in a supply-constrained year is the normal one.

| Business / competence | The only maker of EUV systems, selling to a few chipmakers whose spending is cyclical.[1] | Export rules and customers' budgets are outside its control.[10] | Fourth-quarter 2026 results. |
| Source of value / critical assumptions | Normalized owner earnings of 12,400 growing at 12% and then 8% a year (derived). | The 2026 level of profit is about 27% higher, and the company's 2030 scenarios assume margins at or above 2026's (derived from [7]). | Gross margin guidance for 2027. |
| Permanent-loss case / protection | The Bear value of about EUR 492 a share: a decade without profit growth and a lower multiple (derived). | Net cash and the EUV position protect the business, not the price paid. | Orders falling while capacity expands. |
| Quality, funding and valuation consistency | A Wide moat, Adequate stewardship and Resilient finances agree with each other; the price does not agree with normalized earnings. | A structurally higher margin would close most of the gap. | The Capital Markets Day on June 10, 2027.[4] |
| Value versus price / possible mispricing | Price 33% above Base, 22% below Bull and 173% above Bear (derived). | The gap disappears if 2026's margin proves normal. | Gross margin each quarter. |
| **Overall / change since prior review** | **Thesis not supported at the current price; first full assessment.** | **The quality of the business is not in doubt; the price paid for it is.** | **Fourth-quarter 2026 results.** |

**Summary:** The weakest assumption in the price is that a record margin, earned while customers cannot get enough machines, is the level to grow from.
A second and third year at these margins with demand still ahead of supply would support the price.
The cyclicality of customers' capital spending is the evidence against it.

### Final Verdict

ASML is the only maker of EUV systems, with high returns, net cash and managers who have reinvested well, selling into a cyclical and politically exposed market.[1][3]
At EUR 1,343.80 per share (Amsterdam close, November 9, 2026) the price stands against Bear/Base/Bull estimated values of EUR 492/EUR 1,012/EUR 1,728, 33% above the Base value (derived from [11]).[11]
These are market-multiple hybrid values, not intrinsic values from a cash DCF, so no margin of safety is claimed.
The judgment is overvalued relative to Base, with Medium confidence: the price is roughly the Base case run from the boom-year profit of 2026 instead of a normalized level.
The decisive risk for a buyer at this price is an ordinary equipment downturn, not the loss of the EUV position.
