# Domino's Pizza -- A Franchise Toll Road Whose Growth Has Slowed, Held by Berkshire

Domino's is the largest pizza company in the world, with more than 22,100 locations in over 90 markets, and earns mostly royalties and supply-chain margins from franchisees.[1]
Berkshire Hathaway owns 3,350,000 shares, 9.96% of the company.[4]
In the third quarter of 2026 US same-store sales grew only 0.3% and international 0.2%, and Joe Jordan replaced Russell Weiner as CEO.[2][3]
**Thesis status:** Thesis not supported at the current price. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed; Base value $263 per share against a $303.09 price.

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed.** Perimeter: the whole company.[1]

| Mechanism | Present / Absent / Unknown | Answer / evidence | Contrary evidence | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Switching costs | Present | Franchisees are locked into the system and its supply chain.[1] | Customers switch freely. | High |
| Network effects | Present | Store density shortens delivery times and lowers cost per order.[1] | Aggregators offer delivery from any restaurant. | Medium |
| Brand, patents or licenses | Present | The largest pizza brand in the world.[1] | Pizza is a commodity product. | High |
| Structural cost advantage | Present | Supply chain scale; supply chain gross margin 12.2% in Q3 2026.[2] | Rivals buy at scale too. | Medium |
| Efficient scale | Present | Fortressed delivery areas limit room for new entrants. | Delivery aggregators reduce the advantage. | Medium |
| Scale economies shared | Present | Food basket pricing to stores rose only 2.2% in Q3 2026.[2] | None. | Medium |

**Summary:** The moat is the store network and supply chain: dense stores make delivery cheaper and faster than rivals can match.[1]
Franchisee profitability is the engine, because profitable franchisees open more stores.[2]
Delivery aggregators are the main threat to the density advantage.

| Force / question | Pressure | Evidence-based answer | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Rivalry: can rivals compete away profit through price or capacity? | High | US same-store sales grew only 0.3% in Q3 2026.[2] | High |
| Entry: what prevents a funded newcomer from winning customers? | Medium | Building a delivery network takes years. | Medium |
| Suppliers: can essential providers capture the margin? | Low | Commodity cheese and flour. | Medium |
| Buyers: can concentrated or price-sensitive customers force concessions? | Medium | Consumers are price-sensitive. | Medium |
| Substitutes: can another solution meet the need more cheaply or better? | High | Any restaurant on a delivery app is a substitute. | Medium |

**Summary:** Rivalry and substitutes press hardest: the consumer can choose any meal from any app.[2]
Domino's answer is value pricing and scale, which keeps traffic but limits price increases.
The moat is real but shallow at the customer level and deep at the franchisee level.

**Summary:** Economics score 5: franchisees fund growth, so Domino's needs little capital.[1]
Protection scores 3 because customers can switch at no cost.

### Final Verdict

Domino's is a high-return franchise business with a narrow but stable moat, adequate management in a CEO transition, and leveraged finances on Watch.[1][2][3]
At the Nasdaq close of $303.09 on 2026-10-15, Bear / Base / Bull cash-DCF values of $137 / $263 / $347 put the price 15.3% above Base and 12.6% below Bull (derived from the Valuation table; price from [6]).
My judgment is overvalued relative to Base, with Medium confidence: the price assumes growth resumes, and leverage magnifies the downside.
A return of US same-store sales above 3% would raise the Base case; a price near $184 would give a 30% margin.
