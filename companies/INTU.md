# Intuit -- A Growing Small-Business Platform, Priced as if AI Will Stop It Growing

Intuit sells TurboTax, QuickBooks, Credit Karma and Mailchimp, and revenue grew from $4,694 million in fiscal 2016 to $21,448 million in fiscal 2026.[1][5]
In 2026 the shares fell on fears that AI will replace tax and accounting software, but fiscal 2027 guidance still calls for revenue growth of 9% to 10%.[2]
At $330.17 the price requires owner cash to grow only about 2.3% a year for five years (derived), below every year of the past decade.
**Thesis status:** Thesis supported with a modest margin. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed; Base value $439 per share against a $330.17 price.

**Identity and basis:** Intuit Inc. (Delaware), common stock INTU on Nasdaq; one class; 267,236 thousand shares outstanding on August 31, 2026.[1]
Reviewed 2026-10-16 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.

**Summary:** Intuit is two businesses: an annual tax toll on US consumers and a subscription ledger for small businesses that is growing faster.[1][2]
Fiscal 2026 operating cash of 8,838 was flattered by a tax law change that cut cash taxes to 281 from 1,408 (derived), so the valuation removes that effect.[1]
The market's question is whether AI makes tax preparation and bookkeeping cheap enough to break Intuit's pricing.

**Summary:** The moat is switching costs in QuickBooks and brand trust in TurboTax.[1]
The 10-K itself warns that AI is intensifying competition and raising customer expectations.[1]
Falling TurboTax units with rising revenue mean price is doing the work there, which is not sustainable forever.[2]

**Summary:** Substitution is the risk the market prices: if AI agents prepare simple returns and books, TurboTax and parts of QuickBooks lose pricing power.
Against that, QuickBooks Online grew 23% in fiscal 2026, so customers were still choosing it.[2]
The evidence shows a strong moat under a new and unmeasured threat.

### Final Verdict

Intuit is a high-quality software business with a narrow but stable moat, adequate management that now counts stock pay as a cost, and resilient finances.[1][2]
At the Nasdaq close of $330.17 on 2026-10-15, Bear / Base / Bull cash-DCF values of $228 / $439 / $640 put the price 24.8% below Base and 44.6% above Bear (derived from the Valuation table; price from [6]).
My judgment is undervalued relative to Base, with Medium confidence: the price requires only about 2.3% growth, but AI substitution is a real and unmeasured threat.
TurboTax revenue falling in the 2027 tax season, or QuickBooks Online growth below 10%, would invalidate the Base case; a price near $307 would give a 30% margin.
