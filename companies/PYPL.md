# PayPal -- Priced at Its No-Growth Value, With a Checkout Franchise That Is Slowly Losing Price

PayPal moves money between 439 million active accounts and the merchants that accept them, and keeps a fee on each payment.[1]
Its volume grows faster than its profit: in the third quarter of 2026 total payment volume rose 6% and transaction margin dollars 2%.[3]
Owner earnings are about USD 4,900 million a year, USD 5.84 per share now outstanding and 9.7% of the share price (derived).
**Thesis status:** Thesis supported. **Confidence:** Low. **Valuation status:** Completed as an earnings proxy; Base value USD 72 per share against a USD 60.05 price.

**Identity and basis:** PayPal Holdings, Inc. (Delaware); common stock PYPL on Nasdaq; one class of common stock, of which 830,658,298 shares were outstanding as of October 21, 2026.[2]
Reviewed 2026-11-10 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
US GAAP, consolidated, fiscal year ending December 31; USD millions unless stated.
**History covered:** fiscal 2015, fiscal 2023 to 2025 and the first nine months of 2026.[1][2][6]

## Business and Method

**Coverage: Assessed.**

| Question | Assessment / evidence |
|:--|:--|
| Who pays, for what, and why do customers return? | Merchants pay a fee on each completed payment so that consumers can pay with one trusted button. In 2025 PayPal processed $1.79 trillion of total payment volume in 25.4 billion payment transactions.[1] |
| Segments, geography and principal competitors | One business with three kinds of product: branded checkout (PayPal and Venmo), unbranded card processing (Braintree) and credit. PayPal describes itself as technology and platform agnostic, so merchants and consumers use it alongside other tools.[1] |
| Main revenue, margin and cash-generation drivers | Volume, the rate kept on it and losses. The transaction take rate fell from 1.64% to 1.58% in a year, and branded checkout volume grew 1% while total volume grew 6%.[3][4] |
| Capital required and who bears financing risk | Little in payments; credit needs funding. Loans and interest receivable held for sale were 3,687 at September 30, 2026, against 1,726 at the end of 2025.[2] |
| Sector-specific measures and historical comparison | Net revenues rose from $9,248 million in 2015 to $33,172 million in 2025, and operating income from 1,461 to 6,065.[1][6] |
| Circle of competence and decisive limitations | The business is understandable. The open question is whether the branded button keeps its place at checkout; the filings give no measure of what it adds for a merchant. |
| Principal valuation method / useful cross-check | Simple DCF on owner earnings: non-GAAP net income, which already bears stock-based compensation. Cross-checks: operating cash flow less capital spending and stock pay, and the growth the price implies. |

**Summary:** PayPal earns a small percentage of a very large flow of payments.
The flow keeps growing, but the percentage has been falling for years, because more of the volume is low-priced processing and less is the branded button.
Profit per share has been held up by cost cuts, lower losses and a shrinking share count.
The accounting point in its favour is that its adjusted earnings do not add back stock pay.

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed.**

| Mechanism | Present / Absent / Unknown | Answer / evidence | Contrary evidence | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Switching costs | Absent | PayPal is platform agnostic, and a checkout page shows several buttons at once.[1] | Stored credentials and habit keep some users. | Medium |
| Network effects | Present, weakened | A two-sided network with 439 million active accounts across approximately 200 markets.[1] | Payment transactions decreased 4% in 2025 while volume rose 7%.[1] | Medium |
| Brand, patents or licenses | Present | A trusted name at checkout, and licences in many markets. | The filings do not measure what the brand adds for a merchant. | Low |
| Structural cost advantage | Absent | Transaction expense was 15,987 of 33,172 of net revenues in 2025, about 48% (derived from [1]). | Scale in fraud detection. | Medium |
| Efficient scale | Absent | Many funded rivals. | None. | High |
| Scale economies shared | Unknown | Lower rates for large merchants look like bargaining, not sharing. | None found. | Low |

**Summary:** The network and the brand are real, and neither stops customers from using rivals at the same checkout.
The evidence of weakening is the price: the rate PayPal keeps on volume has fallen every year shown.
The moat is in the branded button, and that is the part that has stopped growing.

| Force / question | Pressure | Evidence-based answer | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Rivalry: can rivals compete away profit through price or capacity? | High | Branded checkout volume grew 1% in a quarter when total volume grew 6%.[3][4] | Medium |
| Entry: what prevents a funded newcomer from winning customers? | Medium | Licences and trust take years; wallets from phone makers and banks already have both. | Low |
| Suppliers: can essential providers capture the margin? | High | Card networks and banks take transaction expense of about 48% of net revenues (derived from [1]). | Medium |
| Buyers: can concentrated or price-sensitive customers force concessions? | High | The transaction take rate fell from 1.64% to 1.58% in a year.[4] | High |
| Substitutes: can another solution meet the need more cheaply or better? | High | Other wallets, pay-by-bank and buy now, pay later providers sit beside it at checkout. | Medium |

**Summary:** Four of the five forces press on PayPal's price.
Its defence is trust and reach, which slow the erosion and do not stop it.
That is consistent with volume growth well above profit growth.

| Protection /5 | Economics /5 | Durability /5 | Trend /5 | Class / trend | Confidence | Decisive evidence / limitation | Threat / reassessment trigger |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | Weak / narrowing | Medium | Take rate down from 1.64% to 1.58% in a year; branded checkout volume up 1%.[4] | Branded checkout volume turning negative for two quarters. |

**Summary:** The moat is Weak and narrowing: an advantage exists, and it is losing pricing power.
A stable take rate with branded volume growing in line with online spending would justify Narrow.
The trend, not the level of profit, is the reason for caution.

## Valuation

**Coverage: Assessed.** Valuation date 2026-11-10; simple DCF at a 10% required return; USD millions.
**Valuation type: earnings proxy**, cross-checked against cash.
Input line: PayPal, 2026-11-10; owner earnings to common shareholders; V0 4,900; A 0; S 839.5 million; P USD 60.05 (derived).
V0 is the guided 2026 non-GAAP EPS of about $5.60 on about 875 million average diluted shares; S is the 830,658,298 shares outstanding scaled by the third quarter's diluted-to-basic ratio (derived from [2][3][4]).
Cash cross-check: 2025 operating cash flow less capital spending of 852 and stock-based compensation was about 4,562 (derived from [1]).
A is zero: net cash of about $1.6 billion is left out, because part of the cash supports regulated subsidiaries and the uncertain tax liabilities stand against it (assumption).
Owner earnings per share: USD 5.84, a 9.7% yield on the price and about 10.0% on enterprise value (derived).

| Case | g1 / g2 / X | PV years 1-10 | PV terminal | Value/share (earnings proxy) | Price at 30% discount | Price at 50% discount |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Base | 2% / 2% / 12 | 33,113 | 27,635 | 72 | 51 | 36 |
| Bull | 6% / 4% / 15 | 39,213 | 46,138 | 102 | 71 | 51 |
| Bear | -4% / -2% / 8 | 25,478 | 11,139 | 44 | 31 | 22 |

**Summary:** The Base case assumes owner earnings grow 2% a year, about the pace of transaction margin dollars now, with cost savings offsetting a falling take rate (assumption).
The Bear case has the branded button keep losing share so that earnings shrink; the Bull case has branded checkout grow again.
With no growth at all and an exit at ten times, the value is about USD 58 a share, which is about the price (derived).
At USD 60.05 the price implies owner earnings shrinking about 0.7% a year for ten years before the Base exit (derived).
Each 10% on owner earnings moves the Base value by about USD 7.20 a share, and an exit at ten times instead of twelve lowers it by about USD 5.50 (derived).
With flat earnings spent entirely on buybacks at this price, earnings per share would still grow about 10.8% a year (derived).
Confidence is Low: the arithmetic is firm, and the direction of the franchise is the unsettled input.

| Source of value / critical assumptions | Owner earnings of about USD 4,900 million that hold roughly flat, returned mostly through buybacks (derived). | Profit growth in 2026 leaned on lower losses and cost cuts.[3] | Transaction margin dollars excluding interest on customer balances. |

| Quality, funding and valuation consistency | Adequate management, a Weak and narrowing moat, Resilient finances and a price at the no-growth value agree with each other. | A narrowing moat argues for a lower exit multiple than twelve. | The take rate each quarter. |
| Value versus price / possible mispricing | Price 17% below Base, 41% below Bull and 38% above Bear (derived). | An approach of about $60.50 per share was abandoned in 2026, which suggests informed buyers saw no bargain much above this price.[10] | Any new offer, or its absence. |
| **Overall / change since prior review** | **Thesis supported at this price as a no-growth holding; Base value unchanged from the October 2026 research note.** | **The discount to Base is small against the distance to the Bear value.** | **Fourth-quarter 2026 results.** |

**Summary:** The thesis that holds is narrow: at this price an owner is paid about 10% a year if earnings merely stay flat.
It does not rest on a recovery, and it fails if earnings start to shrink.
The evidence to watch is the branded button: its volume, and the rate PayPal keeps on it.

### Final Verdict

PayPal has a real but weakening network, candid managers with a poor record on growth targets, and finances strong enough to keep returning about $6B a year.[1][3][4]
At USD 60.05 per share (Nasdaq close, November 9, 2026) the price stands against Bear/Base/Bull estimated values of USD 44/USD 72/USD 102, 17% below the Base value (derived from [11]).[11]
These are earnings-proxy values, not intrinsic values from a cash DCF, so the gap is not a proven margin of safety.
The judgment is modestly undervalued relative to Base, with Low confidence: the price equals the no-growth value, so the owner is paid for flat earnings and gets any growth free.
The decisive risk is that the branded button keeps losing price and share faster than costs can be cut.
