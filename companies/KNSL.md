# Kinsale Capital -- The Low-Cost Underwriter Entering a Soft Market at a Fair Price

Kinsale writes excess and surplus lines insurance, the hard-to-place risks standard insurers decline, and grew gross written premiums from $188,478 thousand in 2016 to $1,977,171 thousand in 2025.[1][6]
It combines a combined ratio of 75.9% in 2025 with a technology-based low expense ratio, and the founder-CEO owns 4.0% of the company.[1][5]
In 2026 premiums started falling: second-quarter gross written premiums dropped 5.0% as Commercial Property premiums fell 32.7% under heightened competition.[2]
**Thesis status:** Thesis not supported at the current price. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed; Base value $390 per share against a $357.49 price.

**Identity and basis:** Kinsale Capital Group, Inc. (Delaware), common stock KNSL on the NYSE; one class; 22,778,425 shares outstanding at July 17, 2026.[3]
Reviewed 2026-10-16 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
US GAAP consolidated statements; fiscal year ends December 31; amounts in USD thousands in quoted statements and USD millions in derived figures unless stated per share or in percent.[1]
**History covered:** 2016 and 2024-2025 annual statements and the six months to June 30, 2026.[1][2][3][6]

Its edge is cost: the 10-K describes a technology approach that keeps the expense ratio low as the business grows.[1]
Insurance growth needs capital, so the valuation treats part of earnings as reinvested rather than distributable (derived).

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed.** Perimeter: the whole company.[1]

| Structural cost advantage | Present | Expense ratio 20.8% in 2025, supported by in-house technology.[1] | The expense ratio rose to 21.7% in Q2 2026.[2] | High |
| Efficient scale | Absent | Large, fragmented market. | None. | High |
| Scale economies shared | Present | Low costs let Kinsale win business at acceptable prices.[1] | Rivals cut price in the soft market.[2] | Medium |

**Summary:** Kinsale's moat is low cost, the most durable advantage in a commodity business like insurance.[1]
In the A.M. Best surplus lines composite from 2001 to 2024, the average loss ratio was 68.7%, and Kinsale's 2025 loss ratio was 55.1%.[1]
The advantage does not stop the insurance cycle; it lets Kinsale stay profitable when rivals are not.

|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Rivalry: can rivals compete away profit through price or capacity? | High | Heightened competition in Commercial Property cut premiums 32.7% in Q2 2026.[2] | High |
| Entry: what prevents a funded newcomer from winning customers? | Medium | Capital is plentiful in soft markets.[1] | Medium |
| Suppliers: can essential providers capture the margin? | Low | Reinsurers; Kinsale has raised retention.[2] | Medium |
| Buyers: can concentrated or price-sensitive customers force concessions? | Medium | Brokers shop each risk. | Medium |
| Substitutes: can another solution meet the need more cheaply or better? | Low | Admitted insurers decline these risks.[1] | Medium |

**Summary:** Rivalry is the decisive force: soft markets, where insurance capital exceeds demand, push prices down.[1]
Kinsale's response is to write less, which protects margins but slows growth.[2]
Its cost advantage is what lets it keep writing profitably at prices that hurt rivals.

Protection scores only 3 because insurance has no switching costs; the cost edge is real but must be earned every year.
A combined ratio drifting toward 85% would show the edge eroding.

The best time to own an excellent underwriter is when a soft market has pushed the price down; that may be closer, but it is not here yet.

### Final Verdict

Kinsale is an excellent insurer with a narrow, cost-based moat, strong founder management and resilient finances.[1][3][5]
At the NYSE close of $357.49 on 2026-10-15, Bear / Base / Bull earnings-proxy values of $224 / $390 / $638 put the price 8.3% below Base and 59.5% above Bear (derived from the Valuation table; price from [7]).
My judgment is fairly valued, with Medium confidence: quality is high, but a soft market is starting and the price is about 4.0 times book (derived).
A combined ratio above 85% or adverse reserve development would invalidate the Base case; a price near $273 would give a 30% margin.
