# Constellation Brands -- The Modelo and Corona Franchise After Its Growth Stopped

Constellation Brands holds the exclusive right to import, market and sell Modelo, Corona and Pacifico beer in all 50 US states, and beer was 8,315.2 of its 9,139.0 million of fiscal 2026 net sales.[1]
In fiscal 2026 beer net sales fell 3% and depletions fell 2.1%, after the company had promised 2% to 4% beer growth for fiscal 2027 and 2028; fiscal 2027 guidance now calls for beer net sales of -1% to 1%.[1][4][5]
A new CEO, Nicholas Fink, took over on April 13, 2026, and the 10-K names immigration enforcement affecting Hispanic consumers as a risk.[1]
**Thesis status:** Thesis not supported at the current price. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed; Base value $104 per share against a $129.58 price ($137 if capital spending falls to depreciation).

**Identity and basis:** Constellation Brands, Inc. (Delaware), Class A common stock STZ on the NYSE; 169,198,487 Class A and 25,733 Class 1 shares outstanding at September 30, 2026, 169,224,220 in total (derived).[3]
Reviewed 2026-10-16 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
US GAAP consolidated statements; fiscal year ends on the last day of February; fiscal 2026 ended February 28, 2026; amounts in USD millions unless stated per share or in percent.[1]
**History covered:** fiscal 2016 and fiscal 2024-2026 annual statements and the six months to August 31, 2026.[1][3][7]

Capital spending on new Mexican breweries has run well above depreciation, which depresses owner cash today (derived).[1]

**Summary:** The moat is brand plus exclusive rights: no rival can sell Modelo or Corona in the US.[1]
Brands in beer are durable but not immune: total beer volume in the US has been shrinking, and Constellation's growth depended on Hispanic consumers.[1]
Share gains in a falling category protect margins but not volume.[2]

**Summary:** Substitution is the real pressure: the beer category itself is losing occasions, which no brand fully escapes.[1]

### Final Verdict

Constellation Brands is a strong beer franchise with a wide but narrowing moat, adequate management under a new CEO, and resilient but leveraged finances.[1][2][3]
At the NYSE close of $129.58 on 2026-10-15, Bear / Base / Bull cash-DCF values of $48 / $104 / $147 put the price 24.7% above Base and 11.9% below Bull (derived from the Valuation table; price from [8]).
My judgment is fairly valued to overvalued, with Medium confidence: with capital spending at depreciation the Base value rises to about $137 (derived), roughly the price.
Positive beer depletions for a full year would support the Bull case; a price near $73 would give a 30% margin at the lower Base.
