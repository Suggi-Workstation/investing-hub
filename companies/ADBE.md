# Adobe -- Superb Cash Economics, Priced as if AI Will Shrink Them

Adobe sells subscriptions to Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, Acrobat and its Firefly AI models, plus marketing software to enterprises; subscriptions were 96% of fiscal 2025 revenue.[1]
The business earned about 63% after tax on its invested capital in fiscal 2025 (derived) and turns most of that into cash, but its moat rests on trained users and workflows that generative AI makes less necessary.[1]
At the October 15, 2026 close the price assumes owner cash about 28% below today's level at Base growth, which is close to what a slow erosion would produce.
**Thesis status:** Thesis supported, with substitution risk unresolved. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed; Base value $353 per share against a $251.67 price.

**Identity and basis:** Adobe Inc. (Delaware), common stock ADBE on Nasdaq; one share class, no ADR.[1][5]
Reviewed 2026-10-15 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
US GAAP consolidated statements; the fiscal year is a 52- or 53-week year ending on the Friday closest to November 30 (fiscal 2025 ended November 28, 2025); amounts in USD millions unless stated per share or in percent.[1]
**History covered:** fiscal 2016 to 2025 annual statements, the nine months to August 28, 2026, and fiscal 2009 as the downturn reference.[1][2][11]

## Business and Method

**Coverage: Assessed.**

| Question | Assessment / evidence |
|:--|:--|
| Who pays, for what, and why do customers return? | Professionals, businesses and consumers pay subscriptions for creative, document and AI tools, and enterprises for marketing software; Adobe reported 1 billion monthly active users across creativity and productivity in Q3 fiscal 2026.[1][3] |
| Segments, geography and principal competitors | Fiscal 2025 Digital Media revenue 17,649 at a 95% gross margin and Digital Experience 5,864 at 72%; the 10-K names software, AI, hardware, operating-system and social-media rivals, and independently Canva and Figma grow far faster.[1][12][13] |
| Main revenue, margin and cash-generation drivers | Total Adobe ARR 25.20 billion, up 11.5% in fiscal 2025, guided to grow 10.2% in fiscal 2026; operating margin 36.6% and stock pay 8.2% of revenue in fiscal 2025 (derived).[1][6] |
| Capital required and who bears financing risk | Capex was 179 in fiscal 2025; customers prepay, with 7,094 of current deferred revenue in August 2026; owners carry 6,150 of senior notes and 250 of commercial paper.[1][2] |
| Sector-specific measures and historical comparison | Owner cash after capex and stock pay was 5,813 / 5,956 / 5,224 / 6,040 / 7,910 in fiscal 2021-2025 (derived); in fiscal 2009 revenue fell 17.7% and operating income 32.8% (derived).[1][11][16] |
| Circle of competence and decisive limitations | Subscription economics are understandable; how fast AI tools replace paid professional seats cannot be forecast, which is the competence limit. |
| Principal valuation method / useful cross-check | Simple DCF on owner cash after capex and stock pay; cross-check: the owner cash the price implies at Base growth. |

**Summary:** Adobe is a toll on creative and document work: once users learn its tools and store work in its formats, they pay every year, and the product costs little to deliver again.[1]
The right measure is owner cash after capex and stock pay, because non-GAAP earnings exclude 1,942 of annual stock pay that owners bear through dilution.[1]
Fiscal 2009, when boxed-software revenue fell 17.7% (derived), is the only real downturn in the record, and the subscription model has never been tested by a substitute this strong.[11][13]
The competence limit is substitution speed, not accounting.

The DOJ suit over cancellation practices, settled in March 2026, is a reminder that some retention may rest on friction rather than on value alone.[2]
The new CEO's first capital decisions are the trigger to watch.

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed.** Perimeter: Digital Media, 74% of fiscal 2025 revenue and the source of the high returns; Digital Experience is a competitive enterprise-software business.[1]

| Mechanism | Present / Absent / Unknown | Answer / evidence | Contrary evidence | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Switching costs | Present | Trained users, file formats and studio workflows; contracted future revenue was 22.16B in August 2026.[2] | Adobe says its agents reduce the need for traditional onboarding and training.[1] | Medium |
| Network effects | Absent | One user's subscription does not make the product better for another. | Shared file formats create a weak standard effect. | Medium |
| Brand, patents or licenses | Present | Photoshop and Acrobat are category names; Firefly is trained on licensed content and sold as commercially safe.[1] | Canva had over 250M monthly users and 31M payers; Figma grew 48% with 136% net retention.[12][13] | Medium |
| Structural cost advantage | Absent | Software costs little to copy for any funded rival. | None. | High |
| Efficient scale | Absent | The 10-K describes limited barriers to entry.[1] | None. | High |
| Scale economies shared | Absent | No evidence of passing scale savings to customers through lower prices. | None. | Low |

**Summary:** The defense is switching cost plus brand: a professional who knows Photoshop and has years of files keeps paying.[1]
That defense is weakest at the point AI attacks, because a tool that needs no training removes the cost of leaving.[1]
The independent evidence shows rivals growing several times faster than Adobe, though from smaller bases.[12][13]

| Force / question | Pressure | Evidence-based answer | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Rivalry: can rivals compete away profit through price or capacity? | High | Canva, Figma and AI companies compete for new creators and design teams.[1][12][13] | Medium |
| Entry: what prevents a funded newcomer from winning customers? | Medium | Professional workflows and trust protect the core, but the 10-K cites limited barriers to entry.[1] | Medium |
| Suppliers: can essential providers capture the margin? | Medium | Adobe integrates partner AI models alongside its own; model owners could become rivals.[1] | Low |
| Buyers: can concentrated or price-sensitive customers force concessions? | Medium | The 10-K lists customer price sensitivity; enterprise buyers negotiate.[1] | Medium |
| Substitutes: can another solution meet the need more cheaply or better? | High | Generative tools let non-specialists produce work that once needed Adobe software.[1] | Medium |

**Summary:** Substitutes are the strongest pressure, and Adobe's switching costs do not neutralize a substitute that needs no switching skill.[1]
The professional core still pays, and growth guidance of 10.2% shows no collapse.[6]
The benefit of AI could go to model companies and AI-native rivals rather than to Adobe.

**Summary:** Adobe converts profit to cash better than reported earnings show, because customers pay in advance and the business needs almost no fixed capital.[1][2]
The stress assumes operating cash halves to about 5,400 a year (derived), worse than fiscal 2009's 33% fall in operating income, with buybacks stopped and 2,400 of paper and notes repaid without refinancing through January 2028.[2][11]
Cash and investments alone cover every maturity in that window, and the 1.5 billion revolver is undrawn.[2]
Survival is not the question; the question is how much cash the business earns in a decade.

## Investment Thesis

**Coverage: Assessed. Status: Thesis supported. Confidence: Medium.** Thesis tested: Adobe's owner cash holds roughly flat or grows despite AI, so the price offers a margin of safety.

| **Overall / change since prior review** | **Undervalued against Base; first full assessment.** | **Earlier research note only.** | **Reassess at each results release and after the CEO change.** |

**Summary:** The weakest assumption is that AI adds to Adobe's paid base rather than replacing it, and the evidence on that is still early.[3][13]
Buffett's four filters: understandable, excellent economics with a narrowing moat, adequate management, and a price that already discounts some erosion.
The next evidence is fiscal 2027 ARR guidance in December 2026 and the new CEO's first capital decisions.

### Final Verdict

Adobe is an exceptional cash generator with a narrow and narrowing moat, adequate management whose buybacks ignore price, and finances that survive any plausible stress.[1][2]
At the Nasdaq close of $251.67 on 2026-10-15, Bear / Base / Bull values of $148 / $353 / $513 put the price 28.6% below Base estimated intrinsic value, though 70% above Bear (derived from the Valuation table; price from [14]).
My judgment is undervalued relative to Base, with Medium confidence: the price is close to a 30% margin of safety, but Bear shows how much is lost if AI replaces paid seats.
Total Adobe ARR growth falling below 7%, or a cut in subscription prices, would invalidate the Base case.
