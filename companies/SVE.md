# Shareholder Value Beteiligungen -- A Portfolio Wrapper Near Its Bear Value, Whose Discount Pays Nothing Until It Closes

Shareholder Value Beteiligungen (SVB) is a small Frankfurt company that invests its own capital in listed shares and reports a net asset value (NAV) per share each month.[3][4]
NAV per share was EUR 130.07 at 31 October 2026, and the share price of EUR 87.50 is about 33% below it (derived from [3][7]).
No owner-earnings figure is defensible for a vehicle like this: an owner earns the growth of NAV after costs, plus or minus the change in the discount.
**Thesis status:** Investigate. **Confidence:** Low. **Valuation status:** Completed on adjusted NAV, with the holdings taken at market prices; Base value EUR 108 per share against a EUR 87.50 price.

**Identity and basis:** Shareholder Value Beteiligungen AG (Germany); registered shares SVE, ISIN DE000A168205, in the Basic Board of the Frankfurt Stock Exchange's open market.[4]
One share class; 697,500 shares issued, of which 20,471 are treasury shares, leaving 677,029 outstanding (derived from [2][4]).
Reviewed 2026-11-10 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
German Commercial Code (HGB), single-entity accounts, fiscal year equal to the calendar year; euros.
**History covered:** 2025 and the first half of 2026, with NAV to 31 October 2026 and the company's own record since 2001.[1][2][3]

## Business and Method

**Coverage: Assessed.**

| Question | Assessment / evidence |
|:--|:--|
| Who pays, for what, and why do customers return? | Nobody: SVB has no customers. It earns dividends and gains on its own holdings; income from securities (dividends) was EUR 884 thousand in 2025.[1] |
| Segments, geography and principal competitors | One portfolio. The ten largest holdings were 75.2% of it at the end of 2025, led by Armira (Sartorius AG) at 15.5%, FamiCord at 12.2%, secunet at 10.2% and Acceleratio Topco (NIQ) at 9.9%.[1] An owner's alternative is to buy listed shares directly or through an index fund. |
| Main revenue, margin and cash-generation drivers | The market value of the holdings, the running costs and the discount at which SVB's own shares trade. NAV per share rose 13.05% from the start of 2026 to EUR 130.07 at 31 October 2026.[3] |
| Capital required and who bears financing risk | None beyond the portfolio: there were no liabilities to banks at the half-year, and a credit facility of EUR 12,500 thousand was available at the end of 2025.[1][2] |
| Sector-specific measures and historical comparison | NAV per share, its growth against the market, and the discount. The annualized return since the first listing in 2001 is 8.4%; in 2025 NAV rose 11.1% while the DAX rose 23.0% and the SDAX 25.3%.[1] |
| Circle of competence and decisive limitations | The wrapper is simple. Its holdings are not valued here: NAV takes them at market prices, and the company states that the figure is not based on audited accounts.[4] |
| Principal valuation method / useful cross-check | Adjusted NAV: published NAV less a haircut on positions held through private vehicles, capitalized running costs and potential option dilution. Simple DCF does not fit a portfolio wrapper. Cross-check: the discount an owner needs after ten years to earn 10% a year. |

**Summary:** SVB is a listed pot of other companies' shares, managed by a small team for a running cost of about 1% of NAV a year (derived from [2][3]).
What an owner gets is the portfolio's return after those costs, at whatever discount the market then applies.
The accounting trap is the income statement: under German rules it records sales and write-downs, not the change in the portfolio's value.
NAV is the measure that tracks owners' wealth, and it is the company's own unaudited figure.

## Valuation

**Coverage: Assessed**, with one decisive limitation: the holdings are taken at market prices and are not valued independently.
Valuation date 2026-11-10; method adjusted NAV; euros per share; NAV per share EUR 130.07 at 31 October 2026.[3]
Shares outstanding: 677,029 (derived from [2][4]).
Simple DCF: Not applicable -- a portfolio wrapper has no operating cash flow to project.
**Owner earnings: no defensible figure.** The discount to NAV is reported instead: about 33% at EUR 87.50 (derived from [3][7]).
Running costs are EUR 920 thousand a year at the first-half rate, about 1.04% of NAV, and are capitalized at ten times: EUR 13.58 a share (derived from [2][3]).
Option dilution assumes exercise at today's share price: EUR 3.47 a share if all 60,000 options are exercised (derived from [6]).

| Case | Private vehicles counted at | Listed holdings counted at | Costs and options deducted (EUR/share) | Value/share (adjusted NAV, EUR) | Price at 30% discount | Price at 50% discount |
|:--|:--|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Base | 80% of their marks | 100% | 15.31 | 108 | 76 | 54 |
| Bull | 100% | 100% | 0.00 | 130 | 91 | 65 |
| Bear | 60% | 90% | 17.05 | 90 | 63 | 45 |

**Summary:** The Bull case is the published NAV and assumes the managers earn back their costs (assumption).
The Base case cuts the two private vehicles, 25.4% of the portfolio at the last disclosed date, by a fifth for the time and uncertainty of getting the money out, and deducts costs and half the options (derived from [1]).
The Bear case also cuts the listed holdings by a tenth, because large positions in small companies cannot be sold at the quoted price (assumption).
At EUR 87.50 the price is 19% below Base, 33% below Bull and 3% below Bear (derived).
Cross-check: if NAV keeps growing at its record of 8.4% a year, a buyer at EUR 87.50 earns 10% a year over ten years only if the discount narrows from about 33% to about 22%; with an unchanged discount the return is 8.4% (derived).
Confidence is Low: the arithmetic is simple, but the private vehicles' current weight is not disclosed and the holdings' own value is not tested here.

**Coverage: Assessed. Thesis status: Investigate. Confidence: Low.**

| Area | Conclusion / decisive evidence | Contrary evidence / limitation | Review trigger / date |

| Business / competence | A wrapper around a concentrated portfolio of mostly listed small and mid-sized companies.[1] | The holdings themselves are not assessed here. | The annual report for 2026. |
| Source of value / critical assumptions | NAV of EUR 130.07 a share, bought at about 33% less (derived from [3][7]). | NAV rose 7.68% in October 2026 alone, so it can fall as fast.[3] | Each monthly NAV release. |
| Permanent-loss case / protection | NAV marks prove too high and the discount stays: about EUR 90 a share (derived). | No debt, so no forced selling.[2] | Write-downs on the private vehicles. |
| Quality, funding and valuation consistency | No moat, Weak stewardship by a narrow margin, Resilient finances and a price near the Bear value agree with each other. | A wide discount on an ordinary wrapper can last indefinitely. | A second buyback, or none within two years. |
| Value versus price / possible mispricing | Price 19% below Base and 3% below Bear (derived). | The discount widened from around 29.2% at the end of 2025 while NAV rose.[1] | The discount at each year end. |
| **Overall / change since prior review** | **Investigate; first full assessment.** | **Decisive missing evidence: the value of the holdings and the current weight of the private vehicles.** | **The 2026 annual report.** |

**Summary:** The weakest assumption is that a discount to NAV is a margin of safety.
It is one only if the NAV can be realized or the discount closes, and SVB's record gives no evidence that either will happen soon.
What would settle the question is an independent look at the five largest holdings, and evidence that buybacks below NAV will be repeated.

### Final Verdict

Shareholder Value Beteiligungen is a debt-free investment wrapper with no moat of its own, candid managers with weak incentives, and a long record of NAV growth below its own target.[1][2]
At EUR 87.50 per share (Xetra close, November 9, 2026) the price stands against Bear/Base/Bull estimated values of EUR 90/EUR 108/EUR 130, 19% below the Base value (derived from [7]).[7]
These are adjusted net asset values built on market prices of the holdings, not intrinsic values, so no margin of safety is claimed.
The judgment is undervalued relative to Base, with Low confidence: the price sits near the Bear value, but an owner is paid only if NAV grows faster than its record or the discount narrows.
The decisive risk is a discount that never closes on a portfolio that compounds below 10%.
