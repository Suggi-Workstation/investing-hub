# Prosus -- Tencent Held Below the Value of the Stake, With a Controlling Shareholder Deciding What Happens to It

Prosus is a holding company whose main asset is a stake in Tencent, beside a portfolio of food-delivery, classifieds, payments and travel businesses.[3][4]
Each Prosus share carries about 0.98 Tencent shares, and the price is about 79% of what those Tencent shares cost in the market, before counting any other asset (derived from [3][9][10]).
Look-through owner earnings are estimated here at about EUR 3.11 a share, 10.1% of the price, but only about EUR 0.56 a share reaches Prosus as free cash flow (derived).
**Thesis status:** Thesis supported. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed as a sum of parts with Tencent on an earnings proxy; Base value EUR 62 per share against a EUR 30.79 price.

**Identity and basis:** Prosus N.V. (Netherlands); ordinary shares N, PRX on Euronext Amsterdam; a subsidiary of Naspers Limited, which holds 75.66% of the votes with a 43.42% economic interest.[1]
Net total shares in issue as at 20 November 2026: 2 072.0 million.[3]
Reviewed 2026-11-25 by Neo after the half-year to 30 September 2026 and Tencent's third quarter; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
IFRS, consolidated, fiscal year ending 31 March; US dollars unless stated; values per ordinary share N in euros.
**History covered:** fiscal 2026 and the first half of fiscal 2027 for Prosus; 2021 to 2025 and the first three quarters of 2026 for Tencent.[2][4][6][8]

**Summary:** Buying Prosus is buying Tencent through a second company that also runs businesses of its own and decides what to do with the cash.
The Tencent stake supplies about 69% of the published asset value (derived from [3]).
The measures that matter are therefore Tencent's earning power, the gap between the Prosus price and its holdings, and what management does with the gap.
The accounting trap is Prosus's reported earnings: most of them are Tencent's retained profit, which Prosus does not receive.

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed** for Tencent, which supplies most of the value; **Limited** for the operating businesses.

| Brand, patents or licenses | Present, limited | Game titles and licences in China. | Games are hit-driven and licences are in the regulator's gift. | Medium |
| Structural cost advantage | Unknown | None shown at Prosus or its businesses. | Operating businesses need continuing investment.[4] | Low |

| Scale economies shared | Absent | No evidence. | None found. | Low |

**Summary:** The protection Prosus owners rely on is Tencent's: a messaging network that advertisers pay more to reach each year.
Prosus's own businesses are local marketplaces whose positions have to be defended with spending, as iFood showed in the half.
The moat belongs to an asset Prosus does not control, which is the central feature of the holding.

**Summary:** The strongest pressures sit in the parts of the portfolio that carry the least value.
For Tencent the open question is a substitute, AI assistants, and Tencent is spending heavily to be the one that supplies it.
Chinese regulation applies to all of it and cannot be diversified away by holding Tencent through a Dutch company.

Sustained profit growth at Tencent through the AI investment period would restore the higher score.

## Valuation

**Coverage: Assessed.** Prices of 2026-11-24 and the asset summary of 25 November 2026; sum of parts in euros per ordinary share N; 2 072.0 million shares; ECB rates of 2026-11-24.[3][9][11]
Simple DCF: Not applicable to Prosus itself, a holding company; it is applied to Tencent.
**Tencent, valuation type: earnings proxy.** V0 is 214,853 (RMB million): non-IFRS profit attributable of the first nine months of 2026 at an annual rate, less share-based compensation of 31,859 and the 2025 share of associates' profit of 23,740, which is counted in the investments instead (derived from [6][7][8]).
A deducts net debt of RMB13.7 billion and adds the investee holdings at a discount; S is about 9,157 million diluted shares (derived from [8]).
Tencent's capital spending is now far above its depreciation, so these owner earnings are not reconciled to free cash flow: Tencent recorded negative free cash flow of RMB69.1 billion for the third quarter of 2026.[8]

**Summary:** The weakest assumption is not Tencent's value but the route by which a Prosus owner receives it.
If the discount never closes, the owner earns Tencent's returns less holding costs and any value lost on acquisitions, improved by repurchases made below value.
The half showed both routes at work: repurchases for a total consideration of US$2.5bn, and US$344m of investment in iFood and JET.[4][5]
The evidence that would weaken the thesis is a shift of cash from repurchases to acquisitions; the evidence that would break it is lasting damage to Tencent's earning power from AI capital that does not earn its cost.

### Final Verdict

Prosus owns a stake in a business with a real network moat, is run by managers who have used the discount for their owners through large buybacks, and is financially safe, but it is controlled by Naspers, keeps acquiring businesses of uncertain value and is spending to defend iFood.[1][3][4]
At EUR 30.79 per share (Amsterdam close, November 24, 2026) the price stands against Bear/Base/Bull estimated values of EUR 38/EUR 62/EUR 87, 50% below the Base value (derived from [9]).[9]
These are sum-of-parts values resting on an earnings proxy for Tencent, not intrinsic values from a cash DCF, so the gap is a discount to an estimate and not a proven margin of safety.
The judgment is undervalued relative to Base, with Medium confidence: the price is below the market value of the Tencent shares alone after all debt and capitalized costs.
The decisive risk is that the discount persists while Tencent's cash goes into AI capital that does not earn its cost.
