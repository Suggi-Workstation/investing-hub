# Prosus -- Tencent Held Below the Value of the Stake, With a Controlling Shareholder Deciding What Happens to It

Prosus is a holding company whose main asset is a stake in Tencent, beside a portfolio of food-delivery, classifieds, payments and travel businesses.[2][3]
Each Prosus share carries about 0.98 Tencent shares, and the price is about 80% of what those Tencent shares cost in the market, before counting any other asset (derived from [3][7][8]).
Look-through owner earnings are estimated here at about EUR 3.21 a share, 8.6% of the price, but only about EUR 0.64 a share reaches Prosus as free cash flow (derived).
**Thesis status:** Thesis supported. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed as a sum of parts with Tencent on an earnings proxy; Base value EUR 61 per share against a EUR 37.34 price.

**Identity and basis:** Prosus N.V. (Netherlands); ordinary shares N, PRX on Euronext Amsterdam; a subsidiary of Naspers Limited, which holds 75.66% of the votes with a 43.42% economic interest.[1]
Net total shares in issue as at 30 September 2026: 2091.1 million.[3]
Reviewed 2026-11-10 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
IFRS, consolidated, fiscal year ending 31 March; US dollars unless stated; values per ordinary share N in euros.
**History covered:** fiscal 2026 for Prosus; 2021 to 2025 and the second quarter of 2026 for Tencent.[2][4][5]

**Summary:** Buying Prosus is buying Tencent through a second company that also runs businesses of its own and decides what to do with the cash.
The Tencent stake supplies about three-quarters of the published asset value (derived from [3]).
The measures that matter are therefore Tencent's earning power, the gap between the Prosus price and its holdings, and what management does with the gap.
The accounting trap is Prosus's reported earnings: most of them are Tencent's retained profit, which Prosus does not receive.

**Summary:** The protection Prosus owners rely on is Tencent's: a messaging network that advertisers pay more to reach each year.
Prosus's own businesses are local marketplaces whose positions have to be defended with spending.
The moat belongs to an asset Prosus does not control, which is the central feature of the holding.

**Summary:** The strongest pressures sit in the parts of the portfolio that carry the least value.
For Tencent the open question is a substitute, AI assistants, and Tencent is spending heavily to be the one that supplies it.
Chinese regulation applies to all of it and cannot be diversified away by holding Tencent through a Dutch company.

**Summary:** Other assets are the published marks of US$40.1 billion outside Tencent, cut because the unlisted marks are about 53 times the operating businesses' adjusted EBIT (derived from [2][3]).

| Value versus price / possible mispricing | Price 39% below Base, 56% below Bull and 2% below Bear (derived). | A holding-company discount is not a margin of safety unless it closes or the cash is distributed. | The pace of repurchases against Tencent sales. |
| **Overall / change since prior review** | **Thesis supported; first full assessment.** | **The return depends on Tencent and on management continuing to buy back shares.** | **Prosus's half-year results.** |

**Summary:** The weakest assumption is not Tencent's value but the route by which a Prosus owner receives it.
If the discount never closes, the owner earns Tencent's returns less holding costs and any value lost on acquisitions, improved by repurchases made below value.
The evidence that would weaken the thesis is a shift of cash from repurchases to acquisitions; the evidence that would break it is lasting damage to Tencent's earning power.

### Final Verdict

Prosus owns a stake in a business with a real network moat, is run by managers who have used the discount for their owners through large buybacks, and is financially safe, but it is controlled by Naspers and keeps acquiring businesses of uncertain value.[1][2][3]
At EUR 37.34 per share (Amsterdam close, November 9, 2026) the price stands against Bear/Base/Bull estimated values of EUR 38/EUR 61/EUR 86, 39% below the Base value (derived from [7]).[7]
These are sum-of-parts values resting on an earnings proxy for Tencent, not intrinsic values from a cash DCF, so the gap is a discount to an estimate and not a proven margin of safety.
The judgment is undervalued relative to Base, with Medium confidence: the price is below the market value of the Tencent shares alone after all debt and capitalized costs.
The decisive risk is that the discount persists while Tencent's cash goes into acquisitions instead of repurchases.
