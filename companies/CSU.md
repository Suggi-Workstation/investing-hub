# Constellation Software -- A Wonderful Reinvestment Machine, Priced for Returns Close to Its Old Hurdle

Constellation buys and keeps small vertical-market software businesses, hundreds of narrow franchises whose customers pay maintenance in advance.[1][3]
Its value depends on one number: the return earned on the roughly two billion dollars of cash it reinvests in acquisitions each year.
At the October 15, 2026 close the price implies about a 10.6% return on new acquisitions, so a modest discount exists only if the old discipline survives the founder's departure.
**Thesis status:** Thesis supported. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed; Base value USD 2,617 (CAD 3,718) per share against a USD 2,311 (CAD 3,283.76) price.

**Identity and basis:** Constellation Software Inc. (Ontario), common shares CSU on the Toronto Stock Exchange; one share class, 21,191,530 shares outstanding; no ADR; Topicus.com and Lumine Group are listed subsidiaries it controls.[1][3]
Reviewed 2026-10-15 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
IFRS consolidated statements in US dollars, December year-end; amounts in USD millions unless stated per share or in percent; the share price is in Canadian dollars.[1][9]
It is converted at the ECB reference currency rates of 2026-10-15, USD 1.1208 and CAD 1.5923 per euro.[10]
**History covered:** 2006-2016 from the President's letters, 2015-2016 and 2024-2025 results, and the six months to June 30, 2026.[1][2][3][5][6][8]

## Business and Method

**Coverage: Assessed.**

| Question | Assessment / evidence |
|:--|:--|
| Who pays, for what, and why do customers return? | Municipalities, utilities, hospitals, schools and niche industries pay for software built around their workflows; maintenance and other recurring revenue was 8,700 of 11,623 in 2025 (derived 75%).[2] |
| Segments, geography and principal competitors | Six operating groups report as one segment; Topicus (Europe) and Lumine (communications) are controlled listed subsidiaries; rivals for acquisitions are private equity firms with far larger funds.[3][6] |
| Main revenue, margin and cash-generation drivers | Revenue grew 15% in 2025, 4% organically; free cash flow available to shareholders (FCFA2S) was 1,683.[2] |
| Capital required and who bears financing risk | Operating businesses run on negative working capital, while the group reinvests nearly all its cash in acquisitions; net debt was 1,309 at June 2026, 2,724 of it without recourse to the parent.[3] |
| Sector-specific measures and historical comparison | Return on average invested capital ran 21%-38% in 2006-2015, while organic growth ranged from -3% to 8%.[5] |
| Circle of competence and decisive limitations | The model is understandable; the return on future acquisitions under new leadership cannot be observed in advance. |
| Principal valuation method / useful cross-check | Simple DCF with explicit reinvestment: owner cash grows by organic growth plus the return on reinvested cash; cross-check: the reinvestment return the price implies. |

**Summary:** Constellation is two businesses: hundreds of small, sticky software franchises, and a capital-allocation engine that reinvests their cash.[1][3]
The right measure is FCFA2S, the company's own figure for cash available to common shareholders after interest, leases and minority holders.[2]
Reported net income is a poor guide, because acquisition amortization of 1,182 in 2025 depresses it (derived from [2]); FCFA2S was more than three times net income attributable to shareholders in 2025.[2]
The competence limit is not the businesses but the price of the next acquisition.

The new President has run operating groups for decades but has no published capital-allocation record as head of the company.[7]

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed.** Perimeter: the portfolio of operating businesses (the source of cash) and the acquisition engine (the source of growth), judged separately.[3]

| Mechanism | Present / Absent / Unknown | Answer / evidence | Contrary evidence | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Switching costs | Present | Mission-critical niche software with prepaid maintenance; recurring revenue outside Altera grew organically 4%-6% in each quarter from mid-2024 to mid-2026.[3] | Altera's recurring revenue fell 19% organically in Q2 2026.[3] | High |
| Network effects | Absent | Each niche product stands alone. | None. | High |
| Brand, patents or licenses | Present | It offers sellers permanent ownership, while most competitors flip acquisitions within 3-7 years.[4] | Brokers invited it into only 16% of large-deal processes over five years.[4] | Medium |
| Structural cost advantage | Present | Decentralized groups with low overhead, autonomy and shared knowledge.[4] | The 2015 letter shows bonuses consuming profit when ROIC ran very high.[5] | Medium |
| Efficient scale | Present | Small niches rarely support many vendors (interpretation). | Leonard says AI tools are widely available to all.[11] | Medium |
| Scale economies shared | Unknown | No measured pass-through to customers. | None. | Low |

**Summary:** The operating moat is strong and local: a municipality or hospital rarely replaces software that runs its daily work.[3][4]
The acquisition engine has a weaker moat, because private equity competes for the same targets and pushes up purchase multiples.[6]
The two must be judged separately: the first protects existing cash, the second decides future growth.

| Force / question | Pressure | Evidence-based answer | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Rivalry: can rivals compete away profit through price or capacity? | Low | Within each niche, incumbents rarely compete on price. | Medium |
| Entry: what prevents a funded newcomer from winning customers? | Low | Rebuilding decades of workflow knowledge for a small market rarely pays. | Medium |
| Suppliers: can essential providers capture the margin? | Low | Staff are the main input; no supplier controls the businesses. | Medium |
| Buyers: can concentrated or price-sensitive customers force concessions? | Medium | Public-sector buyers run tenders, but switching risk restrains them.[3] | Low |
| Substitutes: can another solution meet the need more cheaply or better? | Medium | AI could lower the cost of building niche software; Leonard argued the edge is workflow knowledge, not code.[11] | Low |

**Summary:** The forces acting on the operating businesses are mild, which is why their recurring revenue keeps growing outside Altera.[3]
The real pressure is on the acquisition side, where competition and size push returns toward the cost of capital.[4][6]
AI is the long-run unknown for both.

**Summary:** The operating moat earns Wide: protection, economics and durability each score at least 4 on a decade of evidence.[3][5]
The trend is stable rather than strengthening because organic growth has slowed and the acquisition engine faces lower returns.[3][6]
A portfolio-wide fall in organic recurring growth below zero would lower durability.

| Growth and reinvestment returns | Net investing 106%-112% of FCFA2S in 2024-2025; FCFA2S rose 211 in 2025 on 1,567 invested in 2024, about 13% (derived).[2] | Adequate | Low | One-year ratios are noisy. |
| **Overall** | **Headroom about 1,440 at the stress minimum.** | **Resilient** | **Medium** | **Debt-funded acquisitions at lower hurdles.** |

**Summary:** The businesses throw off cash every year, and customers prepay maintenance, so liquidity rests on recurring revenue rather than on borrowing.[2][3]
The stress assumes acquisitions stop, cash generation halves, dividends continue and 1,804 of commitments due within a year plus a quarter of those due in one to five years are paid without new borrowing.[3]
Cash still ends about 1,440 above a 1,000 floor, and stopping acquisitions alone would release about 2,000 a year (derived).
The weakness is not solvency but the rising share of debt-funded growth.

## Valuation

**Coverage: Assessed;** all values reproduced by a second, independent calculation. Simple DCF at the fixed 10%, valued 2026-10-15 in USD, for common shareholders after minority interests.
**Input line:** V0 = 2,305: FCFA2S of 2,030 in the twelve months to June 2026, plus 207 of IRGA revaluation charges it deducts (the liability is deducted in A instead) and 68 of after-tax net interest (derived).[2][3]
A = cash 2,886 less debt 4,196 less acquisition holdbacks 553 less the IRGA put liability 1,186 = -3,049 (derived); minority shares of subsidiary net debt are ignored, which understates value by about USD 26 a share.[3]
S = 21,191,530 shares on August 11, 2026; no dilutive instruments.[3]
P = USD 2,311.40 (derived): TSX close CAD 3,283.76 on 2026-10-15 converted at the ECB rates of USD 1.1208 and CAD 1.5923 per euro.[9][10] **Type:** Cash DCF.
Model: owner cash grows by organic growth o plus the return R on the 95% of cash reinvested; owners receive the 5% not reinvested each year, plus the exit at X times year-10 owner cash.

| Case | g1 / g2 / X | PV years 1-10 | PV terminal | Value/share (USD, Cash DCF) | Price at 30% discount | Price at 50% discount |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Base | 15.9% / 14.0% / 16 | 1,506.0 | 57,005.4 | 2,617 | 1,832 | 1,309 |
| Bull | 22.1% / 19.3% / 20 | 2,041.9 | 116,317.3 | 5,441 | 3,809 | 2,721 |
| Bear | 9.6% / 8.7% / 11 | 1,115.3 | 23,406.6 | 1,013 | 709 | 507 |

**Summary:** Base assumes 3.5% organic growth and 13% returns on acquisitions falling to 11%; Bull assumes 5% and 18%-15%; Bear assumes 2% and 8%-7%, below the 10% hurdle (assumptions).
All three sit below the 21%-38% return on invested capital of 2006-2015.[5]
Base moves within USD 1,837-2,281 under the stress tests: X of 14 gives 2,281 and 12 gives 1,945; returns 2 points lower give 2,198 and 4 points lower 1,837.
With no acquisitions and all cash paid out, the value would be about USD 1,593 a share at 3.5% organic growth (derived).
At 3.5% organic growth, 95% reinvestment and X of 16 the price implies a 10.6% return on new acquisitions, and 12.2% at X of 14 (derived).
Terminal value is 97% of operating value in every case, because almost all cash is reinvested, so the result depends on the return on reinvestment and the exit multiple.

## Investment Thesis

**Coverage: Assessed. Status: Thesis supported. Confidence: Medium.** Thesis tested: Constellation can keep reinvesting its cash at returns above 10%, so the price offers a margin of safety.

| Area | Conclusion / decisive evidence | Contrary evidence / limitation | Review trigger / date |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Business / competence | Understandable portfolio of sticky niche software.[3] | AI could cheapen niche software.[11] | Recurring organic growth each quarter. |
| Source of value / critical assumptions | Reinvestment returns above 10% on about 2,000 a year (derived).[2][3] | Leonard expected returns to fall as deployment rose.[5][6] | FCFA2S growth relative to prior-year investment. |
| Permanent-loss case / protection | Returns near 8% (Bear, USD 1,013) would cut value by more than half. | Strong balance sheet; stopping acquisitions releases cash. | Large acquisitions at low hurdles. |
| Quality, funding and valuation consistency | Wide moat, Strong management and Resilient finances fit a Base with lower returns than the past. | The successor's allocation record is one year long.[7] | Annual results, March 2027. |
| Value versus price / possible mispricing | Price is 11.7% below Base and 57.5% below Bull. | The price already assumes a return above 10%. | Price below USD 1,832 (CAD 2,603), the 30% margin level. |
| **Overall / change since prior review** | **Modestly undervalued; first full assessment.** | **Earlier research note only, no valuation.** | **Reassess at each results release.** |

**Summary:** The weakest assumption is that the return on new capital stays above the 10% discount rate (assumption).
The founder who defended the hurdle rate has left.[4][7]
Buffett's four filters: understandable, a wide moat with a stable trend, strong management, and a price only modestly below value.
The next evidence is the relation between FCFA2S growth and the cash invested a year earlier, reported each quarter.[2][3]

### Final Verdict

Constellation is a wonderful business with a wide moat, the strongest management of the five reviewed and resilient finances.[3][4][5]
At the TSX close of CAD 3,283.76 (USD 2,311) on 2026-10-15, Bear / Base / Bull values of USD 1,013 / 2,617 / 5,441 put the price 11.7% below Base estimated intrinsic value (derived from the Valuation table; price from [9][10]).
My judgment is modestly undervalued relative to Base, with Medium confidence: the price implies about a 10.6% return on new acquisitions, so the margin of safety is the gap between that and whatever the successor team earns.
Returns on new capital falling below 10%, visible as FCFA2S growth lagging prior investment, would invalidate the Base case.
