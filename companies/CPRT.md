# Copart -- A Wonderful Auction Network in a Pause, Still Priced for Growth

Copart runs online auctions for wrecked and used vehicles, mostly for insurers, with operations at over 250 locations in 11 countries.[2]
Revenue grew from 1,268,449 thousand in fiscal 2016 to 4,666,209 thousand in fiscal 2026, but fiscal 2026 revenue grew only 0.4% and net income fell 4.4%.[1][2][5]
The company holds about $4.5 billion of cash and securities and no debt (derived), and in fiscal 2026 bought back stock for the first time in years at a weighted average of $37.63.[1]
**Thesis status:** Thesis not supported at the current price. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed; Base value $26 per share against a $27.75 price.

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed.** Perimeter: the whole company.[1]

| Brand, patents or licenses | Unknown | Zoning for salvage yards may limit new sites (interpretation). | No filing evidence reviewed. | Low |
| Structural cost advantage | Present | Owned land near cities; property and equipment 3,733,220 thousand.[1] | Rivals own land too. | Medium |
| Efficient scale | Present | Two large players in US salvage.[1] | Carvana, Openlane and ACV compete in used cars.[1] | Medium |
| Scale economies shared | Present | Higher returns on salvage passed to insurers.[1] | None. | Medium |

**Summary:** The moat combines a two-sided network with land that is hard to replicate near cities.[1]
The 10-K names the return on salvage as the insurer's main selection criterion, and a bigger buyer pool raises it.[1]
The weak side is the duopoly with IAA: insurers can move volume, and no filing shows Copart's share.

**Summary:** Entry is the weakest force: building a rival yard network would take land, permits and buyers that cannot be bought quickly.
Rivalry with IAA and the bargaining power of large insurers set the limit on prices.[1]
Volume depends on accidents and on how often repair costs exceed car values, which neither side controls.[1]

### Final Verdict

Copart is a wonderful business with a wide and stable moat, strong owner-operators and a debt-free balance sheet with about $4.5 billion of cash and securities (derived).[1][4]
At the Nasdaq close of $27.75 on 2026-10-15, Bear / Base / Bull cash-DCF values of $19 / $26 / $32 put the price 7.1% above Base and 13.9% below Bull (derived from the Valuation table; price from [6]).
My judgment is fairly valued relative to Base, with Medium confidence: the quality is beyond question, but the price still assumes growth resumes.
A second year of falling US volume would lower the Base case; a price near $18 would give a 30% margin.
