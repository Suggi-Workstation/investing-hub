# Comcast -- A Huge Cash Coupon From a Shrinking Broadband Base, Fairly Priced for the Decline

Comcast is the largest US cable broadband provider and owns NBCUniversal and Sky; revenue grew from $80,403 million in 2016 to $123,707 million in 2025.[1][7]
Its core broadband business is losing customers: domestic broadband penetration of homes and businesses passed fell from 49.8% to 47.6% in 2025, as fixed wireless and fiber competitors cut prices.[1]
In June 2026 Comcast announced a tax-free spin-off of NBCUniversal and Sky, expected in mid-2027, and paused its share repurchases.[2][3]
**Thesis status:** Thesis not supported at the current price. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed; Base value $21 per share against a $21.77 price.

**Identity and basis:** Comcast Corporation (Pennsylvania), Class A common stock CMCSA on Nasdaq; 3,539,192,198 Class A and 9,444,375 Class B shares outstanding at July 15, 2026, 3,548,636,573 in total (derived).[3]

The company is splitting itself in two, so the shareholder of 2028 will own two different businesses.[3]

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed.** Perimeter: the whole company, with Connectivity & Platforms the main cash source.[1][2]

| Mechanism | Present / Absent / Unknown | Answer / evidence | Contrary evidence | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|

| Network effects | Absent | None. | None. | High |
| Brand, patents or licenses | Present | Franchise rights 59,365 on the balance sheet; NBCUniversal content brands.[3] | Brands do not stop broadband losses. | Medium |
| Structural cost advantage | Present | Network passing 64,983 thousand homes and businesses.[1] | Wireless carriers reuse mobile networks for fixed wireless.[1] | Medium |
| Efficient scale | Present | Local markets support few wired networks. | Fiber overbuilders and fixed wireless entered.[1] | Medium |
| Scale economies shared | Absent | None. | None. | Medium |

**Summary:** Cable broadband had a local-monopoly moat: one wired network per neighborhood that no rival could afford to duplicate.[1]
Fixed wireless and fiber have broken that structure in many markets, which is why penetration fell.[1]
The moat still produces a 56.7% margin in business services and 37.7% in residential, but both are under pressure.[2]

| Buyers: can concentrated or price-sensitive customers force concessions? | Medium | Households switch to cheaper offers. | Medium |
| Substitutes: can another solution meet the need more cheaply or better? | High | 5G fixed wireless internet.[1] | High |

**Summary:** Substitution and rivalry press together: the substitute is a cheaper wireless connection from companies with existing networks.[1]
Comcast's answer is convergence, selling wireless lines through its broadband base, where penetration is still about 7%.[2]
Total domestic broadband customer net losses were 139,000 in the fourth quarter of 2024 and 181,000 in the fourth quarter of 2025.[4][5]
The moat is narrowing, not gone.

Wireless growth is the main offset to watch.[2]

**Summary:** As an equity bond the coupon is large, but it is a coupon on a leveraged and shrinking principal: the price already assumes about a 2.7% annual decline (derived).[1]
The spin-off may unlock value or simply divide it; the filings do not yet show the debt split.[3]
Buffett's four filters: understandable, eroding economics, controlled management, and a fair price for a melting asset.

### Final Verdict

Comcast is a cash-rich cable and media company with a narrow and narrowing moat, weak controlled governance and resilient but leveraged finances.[1][3]
At the Nasdaq close of $21.77 on 2026-10-15, Bear / Base / Bull cash-DCF values of $10 / $21 / $37 put the price 2.7% above Base and 40.7% below Bull (derived from the Valuation table; price from [8]).
My judgment is fairly valued, with Medium confidence: the market prices the decline I expect, so the large coupon is compensation for shrinkage, not a margin of safety.
Broadband customer losses stopping would support the Bull case; a price near $15 would give a 30% margin.
