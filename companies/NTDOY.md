# Nintendo -- A Durable Franchise Priced for a Much Bigger Cash Stream

Nintendo sells games and the hardware to play them, and its characters sell both.[1]
The business is good and almost debt-free, but its cash earnings swing with the console cycle.[1]
The current share price assumes far more recurring cash than Nintendo has produced over a full cycle.
**Thesis status:** Thesis not supported at the current price. **Confidence:** Medium. **Valuation status:** Completed; Base value 3,656 yen per share against a 7,896 yen price.

**Identity and basis:** Nintendo Co., Ltd.; NTDOY, unsponsored ADR on US OTC, four ADRs per ordinary share; ordinary shares 7974 on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime Market.[1][14][17]
Reviewed 2026-09-29 by Neo; information cutoff as in frontmatter.
Japanese GAAP consolidated statements, March year-end; amounts in JPY billions unless stated as yen per share, units or percentages.
**History covered:** annual key data FY2013-FY2026 and the quarter to June 30, 2026; the quarterly release has no cash-flow statement.[1][2][13]

## Business and Method

**Coverage: Assessed.**

| Question | Assessment / evidence |
|:--|:--|
| Who pays, for what, and why do customers return? | Consumers buy Nintendo hardware, first- and third-party games, digital content and Nintendo Switch Online; its own franchises give them reasons to return, and partners pay for the IP in films and licensing.[1][2] |
| Segments, geography and principal competitors | One reportable segment; FY2026 sales 2,313.1; 77.9% of Q1 FY2027 sales came from outside Japan; rivals are other consoles, PC and smartphone games and other entertainment.[1][2][10] |
| Main revenue, margin and cash-generation drivers | Software mix drives margin: Q1 FY2027 operating profit rose 150.5% on 9.5% lower sales as software share rose, helped by a US tariff refund; digital was 61.5% of platform software sales.[2][3] |
| Capital required and who bears financing risk | Nintendo funds development (R&D 177.9 in FY2026), launch inventory and a self-financed 230 facilities plan; it has no borrowings.[1] |
| Sector-specific measures and historical comparison | Units, digital share, operating margin and cash conversion over a full console cycle; operating profit ranged from -46.4 in FY2014 to 640.6 in FY2021.[12][13] |
| Circle of competence and decisive limitations | The platform-plus-IP model is understandable; hit timing and the cash economics of The Pokemon Company (32% voting rights) are not.[1] |
| Principal valuation method / useful cross-check | Simple DCF on five-year average core cash, with liquid assets and associates added separately; cross-check: the cash and required return implied by the price. |

**Summary:** Nintendo earns most in the years after a successful launch, when software sells into a large installed base.[1][2]
One year's profit is therefore a poor guide, so the valuation uses a five-year average spanning the second half of the Switch cycle and the Switch 2 launch year.
The accounting trap is that net income includes the associates' share, interest and currency gains, which are not operating cash.[1]
The competence limit is hit-making itself, which no model captures.

**Summary:** Management runs the business well and treats owners fairly, but the evidence does not show owner-like capital allocation.[1]
Payouts follow a formula, and surplus cash accumulates in deposits and securities rather than being bought back below value.[1][8]
The one-dollar test passes only on market value: market capitalization rose about 2.2 yen per yen retained since March 2021 (derived from reported PER, EPS and today's price), a value I consider above intrinsic value.[1][12][17]
Outside directors are fewer than half the board, and the one tied to Nintendo's film partner is not independent.[1][20]

## Moat

**Coverage: Assessed.** Perimeter: Nintendo's global game platform and first-party IP; Pokemon is an associate, not owned outright.[1]

| Mechanism | Present / Absent / Unknown | Answer / evidence | Contrary evidence | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Switching costs | Present, limited | Switch 2 plays Switch games, and Nintendo Accounts keep a direct relationship with over 100m annual players.[2][3] | Players can own several platforms; no retention data. | Medium |
| Network effects | Unknown | Other publishers plan a range of Switch 2 titles.[2] | Not measured; publishers release on many platforms. | Low |
| Brand, patents or licenses | Present | 19.86m Switch 2 units in FY2026; Circana measured 1.6m US units in June 2025; the Mario film more than doubled Q1 IP revenue.[1][2][16] | Hits are not guaranteed; FY2013-FY2014 operating losses.[13] | Medium |
| Structural cost advantage | Absent | Nintendo expects rising memory and component costs.[10] | None found. | Medium |
| Efficient scale | Absent | Large contestable market. | None found. | Medium |
| Scale economies shared | Unknown | Not demonstrated. | Recent price revisions raise prices.[11] | Low |

**Summary:** The defense is characters and games people want and cannot get elsewhere, reinforced by a library that carries across hardware generations.[1][3]
Pricing power is being tested now: the Japanese-language Switch 2 rose from 49,980 to 59,980 yen, and the US Switch 2 from USD449.99 to USD499.99.[11]
Nintendo reports solid Japanese sell-through after the increase, but the president linked the share-price fall to market concern about it.[3][10]
Passing on component costs is not yet proof of pricing power.

| Force / question | Pressure | Evidence-based answer | Confidence |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Rivalry: can rivals compete away profit through price or capacity? | High | Other consoles and PC games compete for the same players and spending. | Medium |
| Entry: what prevents a funded newcomer from winning customers? | Medium | Money cannot quickly recreate decades of Nintendo franchises and development know-how. | Medium |
| Suppliers: can essential providers capture the margin? | High | Memory and component costs are rising and forced price revisions.[10][11] | High |
| Buyers: can concentrated or price-sensitive customers force concessions? | Medium | Households are dispersed but price-sensitive and can delay purchases. | Medium |
| Substitutes: can another solution meet the need more cheaply or better? | High | Smartphones, video and film compete for leisure time; Nintendo itself reaches players there.[10] | Medium |

**Summary:** Substitutes and component suppliers are the strongest pressures on profit.[10]
Franchises differentiate the product but cannot make memory cheaper or force a household to buy a new console.
Durability rests on continuing to make games people choose over free alternatives.

**Summary:** The rubric gives Narrow because Economics is 3: returns are high in good years but have been negative in a failed cycle.[13]
The franchises outlast any one console, but owners bear the cost of a failed one.
A second strong Switch 2 year after the price rises would raise confidence in pricing power.

**Coverage: Assessed.** Review period FY2022-FY2026 plus the June 2026 quarter, with FY2013-FY2014 as the downside reference.[1][2][13]

| Cash generation and conversion | Five-year CFO 1,376.5 versus net income 2,103.9 (65%); FY2025 CFO 12.1 after a 333.8 inventory build.[1] | Adequate | Medium | Unsold launch inventory; June inventory rose to 618.0.[2] |

| Liquidity under stress | Two-year stress from June 2026 keeps at least 1,173.4 of usable cash. | Strong | Medium | A far longer failed cycle than FY2013-FY2014.[13] |
| Growth and reinvestment returns | Cash capex averaged 18.4 a year in FY2022-FY2026, about 6% of after-tax operating profit; 230 of new facilities planned.[1] | Adequate | Medium | Facilities and rising R&D fail to raise per-share cash. |
| **Overall** | **Headroom 673.4 above a 500 operating floor at the stress minimum.** | **Watch** | **Medium** | **Cyclical cash, not solvency, is the weakness.** |

**Summary:** Nintendo cannot plausibly be forced into distress: it has no debt and liquid assets of about 2.1 trillion yen.[1][2]
The stress assumes operating cash of -150 and -50 in two years, well below the FY2013-FY2014 lows of -40.4 and -23.1.[13]
It also pays two years of the facilities plan (76.7 a year), lease principal and FY2027's forecast 162 yen dividend, then stops dividends and raises no new financing.[1][2]
Watch reflects cash conversion that swings with the cycle, not survival risk.

## Valuation

**Input line:** V0 = 229.7, the FY2022-FY2026 average of CFO less cash capex less after-tax interest and dividends received.[1]
A = +924.7 before associates: liquid assets 2,110.5 (June cash, deposits and current securities 1,967.9 plus March noncurrent securities excluding associates 142.6) less a 750 operating reserve, a 10% access and tax haircut, pension 25.4, minorities 1.2, leases 7.5 and 190.7 present value of the facilities plan.[1][2]

| Business / competence | An understandable platform-plus-IP business; Switch 2 sold 19.86m units in its first fiscal year.[1] | Hits do not arrive on schedule; FY2013-FY2014 operating losses.[13] | Q2 FY2027 results, November 2026. |
| Source of value / critical assumptions | Characters and a game library produce cash over a full cycle, about 230 a year on average.[1] | Core cash ranged from -49.1 to 409.3 in five years. | FY2027 software sell-through and cash conversion. |
| Permanent-loss case / protection | The risk is overpaying, not insolvency: the price needs about 608 of annual core cash, more than 2.6 times the five-year average. | The balance sheet protects the company, not a buyer's price. | Recurring core cash sustainably above about 400, which would lift Base to about 5,566 yen. |
| Quality, funding and valuation consistency | Narrow moat, Adequate management and Watch finances are consistent with the Base case. | Bull needs a stronger Switch 2 cycle and higher Pokemon value together. | Volume after the September 2026 US and European price rises.[11] |
| Value versus price / possible mispricing | Price is 116% above Base and 50% above Bull. | The market may expect a much bigger Switch 2 cycle, more digital margin or more Pokemon value, or accept about a 5% annual return on my Base cash. | Price near Base value, or evidence of much higher recurring cash. |
| **Overall / change since prior review** | **Not supported at this price; the business case is supported.** | **Initial assessment in this file.** | **Reassess at each results release.** |

**Summary:** The weakest assumption behind the current price is that Switch 2 will produce recurring cash far above any five-year average Nintendo has achieved.[1]
The next decisive evidence is software sell-through and cash conversion after the September price rises.[11]
Buffett's four filters: the business is understandable, economics are favorable but cyclical, management is able and fair, and the price is not below value.

### Final Verdict

Nintendo is a good, debt-free business with a narrow moat and adequate management, but its cash earnings are cyclical and its capital allocation is formulaic.[1]
At the Tokyo close of 7,896 yen on 2026-09-29 at 3:30 PM JST, Bear / Base / Bull values of 2,268 / 3,656 / 5,250 yen put the price 116% above Base estimated intrinsic value and 50% above Bull.[17]
NTDOY at USD12.51 at 2:09 PM New York time the same day is within 0.5% of that price at 157.12 yen per dollar, against values of USD3.61 / 5.82 / 8.35 per ADR.[18][19]
My judgment is overvalued relative to Base, with Medium confidence; a good business is not a bargain at any price.
I would reassess if recurring core cash proves sustainably above about 400 billion yen a year, or if the price falls toward Base value.
